§ 01 · The stage

Grand Tour debutant Brennan out-sprints the field on the Manosque ramps

Koen Bouwman, Diego Uriarte, Sinuhé Fernández and Ethan Hayter formed the day's breakaway on the largely flat run from Monaco. Hayter went solo with 49 km to go and crossed the intermediate sprint first, briefly moving into the provisional overall lead on bonus seconds, with Wout van Aert taking second there in his own bid for the jersey. UAE Team Emirates - XRG let the move go without reacting, and the peloton reeled Hayter in with 34 km remaining.

The finale in Manosque featured uphill ramps rather than a flat run-in. Inside the closing kilometre, Wout van Aert attacked out of a hairpin, drawing an immediate response from Tadej Pogačar; Alessandro Romele bridged across, but the move came back together under the 1 km banner with no cooperation among the three.

Finn Fisher-Black launched the sprint early, then Matthew Brennan came around him. Mads Pedersen kicked late on the rising gradient but could not match Brennan's explosivity, and nobody could follow the 21-year-old to the line — his first Grand Tour stage win. Pau Miquel finished second and Pogačar third, ahead of Pedersen, while Hayter — caught off guard by the sprint after his effort in the breakaway — arrived several seconds down and lost his hold on the race lead.

§ 02 · GC impact

Pogačar keeps the red jersey and extends his cushion

Tadej Pogačar retained the overall lead after Ethan Hayter's provisional breakaway advantage was undone by the finish. Pogačar added 4 bonus seconds at the line and now leads Wout van Aert by 9 seconds and Matthew Brennan by 10, with both having captured bonus seconds of their own during the stage. On the uphill finish, a split cost time to several GC riders including João Almeida, Felix Gall and Matthew Riccitello, who lost 18 seconds at the line.
