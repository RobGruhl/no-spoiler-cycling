§ 01 · The race in 90 seconds

McNulty solos to a stunning first US road title in 33 years

OPENINGThe 273km route ran from Brossard to Montréal, finishing with repeated laps of the Mont-Royal circuit and its two climbs, the Côte de la Camillien-Houde and Côte de Polytechnique — essentially the closing circuit used at the GP de Montréal two weeks earlier. A 16-man break containing Biniam Girmay, Andreas Leknessund, Felix Engelhardt, Henok Mulubrhan, Guillaume Boivin, Emils Liepins and Jamie Meehan escaped early and built a lead of roughly eight and a half minutes, with Belgium, the Netherlands, Mexico and Great Britain sharing the chase. Once the race reached the Mont-Royal circuit, France's Bruno Armirail drove the peloton hard on his own, whittling the gap down lap by lap and fragmenting the break to its final survivors — Leknessund, Mulubrhan, Meehan and Liepins — before it was swallowed up.

UNFOLDSWith the peloton reset, France went on the attack through Valentin Paret-Peintre, Jordan Jegat and Pavel Sivakov, prompting a series of moves from Oscar Onley, Maxim Van Gils, Giulio Ciccone and Ben Healy. Mathieu van der Poel jumped across, then Wout van Aert attacked in turn, drawing Primoz Roglic and Brandon McNulty into the action. Van Aert went again to stop the group being reeled back in, opening a gap with Van der Poel, Roglic, Anthon Charmig and Oscar Onley before Quinn Simmons bridged to make six. Behind, Isaac del Toro accelerated on the Camillien-Houde with Remco Evenepoel and Paul Seixas responding, dragging Ben Healy, Juan Ayuso, Ciccone and Matteo Jorgenson into a chase that kept threatening to close before stalling again.

DECIDEDVan der Poel split the six-man move twice — first shedding Van Aert, Roglic and Charmig with only Simmons and Onley able to follow, then dropping Onley too with just over 50km left to leave himself, Simmons and Roglic clear as Healy chased alone and eventually bridged across. Behind, Evenepoel, Del Toro, Seixas and Van Aert kept attacking and countering each other until a reformed group of around twelve came back together, among them Seixas, Ciccone, Jorgenson, Pidcock, Van der Poel, McNulty and Matthews. From that group, with 33km to go, McNulty attacked. Nobody reacted immediately — his US teammates Simmons and Jorgenson had no reason to chase, and Del Toro was reluctant to tow rivals back toward his own UAE Team Emirates teammate — and the gap passed a minute within a few kilometres.

FINALEMcNulty reached the final lap with around 53 seconds in hand, but Tom Pidcock and Giulio Ciccone cut hard into the gap on the last ascent of the Camillien-Houde, reducing it to roughly 10 seconds as Del Toro, Seixas, Matthews, Jorgenson and Van der Poel stayed close behind. Just as the catch looked inevitable, the chasers hesitated rather than commit their last reserves, and McNulty found more time on the descent and final kilometres. Mauro Schmid, Van der Poel and Evenepoel all launched late moves out of the group, but McNulty held on to cross the line in 6:17:04, 13 seconds clear. Matthews won the sprint from the chase group for silver, with Van der Poel completing the podium in bronze — the first American world road race champion since 1993.

§ 03 · Team-by-team

Who pressed, who missed

United States
McNulty wins, three in the top eight

McNulty's decisive attack came from a group that already contained two US teammates, Quinn Simmons and Matteo Jorgenson, who had spent the race marking moves and staying fresh rather than chasing. "Our plan was to keep myself, Quinn and Matteo as fresh as possible," McNulty said afterward. "We had Quinn up in an earlier move, which kind of let me and Matteo play a bit in the back. We came together again and I chose the right moment to go and never looked back." Simmons finished fourth in the chase-group sprint and Jorgenson eighth, giving the US three riders in the top eight — its best world road race result in decades before McNulty's win itself broke a 33-year drought.

Riders: MCNULTY Brandon, JORGENSON Matteo
Australia
Matthews wins the sprint for silver

Matthews survived several moments where he looked close to being distanced from the front group in the finale, then found the strongest finish of the chasers to take silver — his second career runner-up finish at the Worlds road race.

Riders: MATTHEWS Michael
Netherlands
Van der Poel animates all day, salvages bronze

Van der Poel was one of the race's principal aggressors, launching the key mid-race split that reduced the leading group to himself, Simmons and Roglic, and attacking again in the finale — but he was still in the chase group when McNulty went clear at 33km, and had to settle for bronze after playing much of his hand too early.

Riders: VAN DER POEL Mathieu
Belgium
Evenepoel and Van Aert attack but miss the podium

Van Aert attacked early to help force the race open before easing back to support Evenepoel, who struggled through the middle of the race, faded and reappeared repeatedly, and launched a final attack out of the chase group in the closing kilometres — but neither could force their way onto the podium, with Evenepoel finishing 12th, 25 seconds down.

Riders: EVENEPOEL Remco, VAN AERT Wout
Mexico
Del Toro forces the selection, fifth

Del Toro's repeated accelerations on the Camillien-Houde helped force the finale's decisive selection, and he chased hard on the final lap as McNulty's lead shrank to 10 seconds, but couldn't close it down, finishing fifth in the chase-group sprint.

Riders: DEL TORO Isaac
Great Britain
Pidcock closes hardest, seventh

Recovering from an earlier crash, Pidcock was the most persistent chaser in the finale, repeatedly attacking the group behind McNulty and at one stage distancing Del Toro and Van der Poel, cutting the gap to around 10 seconds on the final climb before it stalled again — finishing seventh.

§ 04 · Rider-by-rider · tracked riders only

How each story played out

🏳️ Brandon McNulty United States
LEADER P1 winner

A trusted domestique for the injured Tadej Pogačar for much of the season, McNulty attacked from a regrouped dozen riders with 33km to go and quickly opened a gap of over a minute. His advantage shrank to around 10 seconds on the final ascent of the Camillien-Houde as Pidcock and Ciccone closed hard, but the chase behind him stalled and he held on to win by 13 seconds, ending a 33-year wait for a US men's road race world champion. "Until the last few hundred metres, I really didn't even believe it," he said. "The last climb up Mount Royal, I don't know how I did it. I was just dying, but I kept pushing."

§ 06 · Aftermath

The first American rainbow jersey since 1993

""

—

""

—

McNulty's win is only the third US men's road race world title, after Greg LeMond (1983, 1989) and Lance Armstrong (1993), and comes with two-time defending champion Tadej Pogačar absent after late-season collarbone surgery. McNulty, a long-time domestique for Pogačar at UAE Team Emirates - XRG, had flown under the radar all season before his solo ride — his best 2026 result had been third at the GP de Montréal on the same circuit two weeks earlier. With Quinn Simmons fourth, Matteo Jorgenson eighth and McNulty on top, it was the strongest US showing at the Worlds road race in decades, arriving less than two years before the Los Angeles 2028 Olympics.
