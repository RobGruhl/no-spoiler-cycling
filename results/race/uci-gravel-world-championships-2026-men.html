UCI Gravel World Championships — Men Elite
2026

Magnus Bak Klaris (Denmark) won a four-up sprint in Nannup, Western Australia to take the rainbow jersey, upsetting defending champion Florian Vermeersch (Belgium) and Great Britain's Connor Swift after his own countryman Mads Würtz Schmidt crashed and remounted to rejoin the front group inside the closing kilometres.

§ 01 · The race in 90 seconds

A kangaroo, a crash and a surprise Danish sprinter

OPENINGRacing the first Gravel World Championships held outside Europe — and the first in the Southern Hemisphere — 144km over the SEVEN Gravel Race route around Nannup, Western Australia, with 3,713m of climbing packed into a string of short, repeated ramps. A reduced field of pros (Florian Vermeersch, Stan Dewulf and Connor Swift the only WorldTour riders on the start list) set off at 10:10am local time. A breakaway of Lukas Pöstlberger (Austria), Jo Pirotte (Belgium) and Jenson Young (Great Britain) went clear early with Dutchman Peter Schulting; Pirotte dropped back, leaving a three-rider move that built a lead of more than three minutes.

UNFOLDSWith roughly 70km left, the chasing group — including Mads Würtz Schmidt, Matthew Beers and Keegan Swenson — narrowly avoided a kangaroo that jumped onto the road in front of them just before the Ellis Creek climb, the toughest of the day. On Ellis Creek, Jonas Lindberg led over Würtz Schmidt and split the group; Swift and Vermeersch followed while Beers, Swenson and the Dutch contingent dug deep to stay in touch. The chase then eased, letting Schulting's leading trio stretch back out to two minutes before Young was dropped from the break 40km from the finish.

DECIDEDWith 22km to go, on the day's decisive climb, Würtz Schmidt attacked out of the reduced group, ending Pöstlberger and Schulting's day out front after 115km in the lead. Only Vermeersch, his countryman Bak Klaris, Swift and a surprising Martin Stosek (Czech Republic) could follow; Vermeersch and Stan Dewulf closed back up to the group just before the final, decisive stretch.

FINALEOn the final climb Würtz Schmidt attacked again, with Swift matching him; Bak Klaris and Vermeersch dug in to hold the wheel. Würtz Schmidt went again with 4km to go and Swift again brought him back, Vermeersch watching the gap grow before closing it down. Inside the closing kilometres Würtz Schmidt crashed on a loose gravel corner, remounted immediately and fought his way back to the front group of four. Swift opened the sprint first, but Bak Klaris came through late to take the rainbow jersey, with Vermeersch second and Swift third; Würtz Schmidt crossed the line fourth. Paul Voss (Germany) led the next group home in fifth, seven seconds back.

§ 06 · Aftermath

A first Southern Hemisphere Worlds crowns a surprise champion

Bak Klaris's win was an upset over pre-race favourite and defending champion Florian Vermeersch, who finished second in his title defence, and over European champion and Unbound Gravel winner Mads Würtz Schmidt, who crashed late while attacking for the win and still recovered to finish fourth. Connor Swift took bronze, the best result of the day for the race's small WorldTour contingent. The men's race followed Marianne Vos's victory in the women's elite race the day before, completing the first Gravel World Championships held outside Europe.
