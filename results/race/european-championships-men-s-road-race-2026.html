§ 01 · The race in 90 seconds

Evenepoel survives a crash to beat Ciccone by millimetres

OPENINGThe 196.3km race left Ljubljana and ran through Škofja Loka, Kranj, Kamnik and Komenda before reaching the Šenčur finishing circuit for five laps including the Možjanca climb (2.1km at around 10%). A five-rider move containing Tiago Antunes, Jakob Söderqvist, Fabian Weiss, Luka Mezgec and Josef Černý built an early lead but never threatened to stay away, with Belgium controlling the peloton through most of the afternoon. The break was caught with 82km to go, and the same phase saw Juan Ayuso lose contact with the front group alongside Julien Bernard and Gijs Leemreize; Ayuso did not finish.

UNFOLDSJan Christen attacked with 81km left, followed by Giulio Pellizzari, but neither move got clear of the Belgian-controlled bunch. Pavel Sivakov had a brief spell on the front without lasting effect. On the penultimate ascent of Možjanca, Belgium set the pace and Remco Evenepoel attacked with Giulio Ciccone following his wheel.

DECIDEDEvenepoel crashed just after the summit of the penultimate climb, losing his front wheel in a corner he had entered too fast. The move briefly left Ciccone isolated at the front, but Belgium's numbers brought the race back together before Evenepoel counter-attacked on the descent a few kilometres later, again with Ciccone in tow. The pair cooperated to the finish, with both of their teammates sitting on the chasing wheels behind. Christian Scaroni attacked twice out of the first chase group in the closing kilometres to secure the bronze medal position on his own.

FINALECiccone attacked on the final corner in Šenčur, taking an inside line for a brief gap and appearing to have the sprint won, but Evenepoel came back around him in the last metres. Neither rider knew the result immediately; both waited several minutes for the photo-finish before Evenepoel's win was confirmed, giving Belgium the title in 4:16:37 with Ciccone credited the same time. Scaroni's second attack held off the first chase group by 59 seconds for third, with Toms Skujiņš leading that group home 14 seconds further back. Primož Roglič, leading Slovenia in Tadej Pogačar's absence, finished 10th at +1:19 without featuring in any of the race's decisive moves.

§ 03 · Team-by-team

Who pressed, who missed

Belgium
Evenepoel wins after a crash

Belgium controlled the race for most of the afternoon and set the pace on the penultimate Možjanca climb for Evenepoel's decisive attack. After Evenepoel crashed just past the summit, the team's numbers brought the race back together rather than letting Ciccone escape alone, and Ramses Debruyne (6th) and Maxim Van Gils (8th) both finished in the first chase group behind the winner. "The race played out exactly as we wanted," Evenepoel said afterward, crediting his teammates for the plan around his late attack.

Riders: EVENEPOEL Remco
Italy
Silver and bronze, gold slips away

Giulio Ciccone matched Evenepoel's late move and very nearly won the sprint, taking the inside line on the final corner before being passed in the last metres — a defeat decided by a photo finish rather than a gap. Christian Scaroni added a second podium place with two accelerations out of the first chase group in the closing kilometres. It extends Italy's run without the European men's road title since its four-year streak ended in 2021, but the near-miss was as close as a result can be.

Riders: CICCONE Giulio, SCARONI Christian
Slovenia
Roglič can't fill the host nation's void

With Tadej Pogačar absent, Primož Roglič carried Slovenia's hopes on home roads but was never part of the race's decisive moves, finishing 10th at +1:19 alongside the first chase group. Jakob Omrzel, the other Slovenian card, finished further back at +2:08. The home crowd around Ljubljana and Šenčur did not get the result it wanted.

Riders: ROGLIČ Primož
France
Bisiaux's day, bigger names miss out

Léo Bisiaux was France's best-placed rider by a wide margin, finishing 9th in the first chase group at +1:18, while senior favourites Romain Grégoire and Kévin Vauquelin both finished in a group at +2:08, well outside the placings that mattered.

Riders: BISIAUX Léo, GRÉGOIRE Romain, VAUQUELIN Kévin
§ 04 · Rider-by-rider · tracked riders only

How each story played out

🇧🇪 Remco Evenepoel Belgium
LEADER INCIDENT winner

Evenepoel attacked on the penultimate ascent of Možjanca with Ciccone on his wheel, then crashed in a corner just past the summit after entering it too fast — a moment that briefly threatened his title bid before Belgium's numbers pulled the race back together. He attacked again on the descent, this time making it stick with Ciccone alongside, and the two cooperated to the finish. Ciccone took the inside line on the final corner and appeared to have won, but Evenepoel came around him in the closing metres; both riders waited several minutes for the photo-finish before his win was confirmed. It completes his collection of major championship jerseys and comes a week after a Worlds result in Montréal he described as having affected him "more mentally than I let on."

🇮🇹 Giulio Ciccone Italy
LEADER P2 same time

Ciccone matched Evenepoel's attack on the penultimate climb and again on the descent after the Belgian's crash, the two riders cooperating the rest of the way to the finish. He took a brief lead with a well-timed inside line on the final corner, but Evenepoel passed him in the last metres; the margin was close enough that neither rider knew the outcome until the official photo-finish was released. It is his second runner-up finish at a major road title decided by the smallest of margins.

§ 06 · Aftermath

A photo-finish title and relief for Evenepoel

Evenepoel had to wait several minutes for the photo-finish to confirm his win, in an arrival so tight that neither he nor Ciccone knew the result immediately. The Belgian said the Montréal Worlds defeat the week before had weighed on him more than he showed publicly, and that completing his set of major championship titles brought major relief. The result came with defending champion Tadej Pogačar not on the Slovenian start list by his own choice, leaving Primož Roglič to lead the home nation; Roglič finished 10th without featuring in the race's decisive moves.
