Stage 7: Vall d'Alba → Aramón Valdelinares
Vuelta a España 2026

Andreas Leknessund soloed to an emotional Uno-X Mobility one-two ahead of teammate Tobias Halland Johannessen at Aramón Valdelinares, while Wout van Aert took third and the green jersey after a day-long attack, and Tadej Pogačar counter-attacked the GC group to stretch his overall lead over Enric Mas from 3:34 to 3:50.

§ 01 · The stage

Leknessund solos to an Uno-X one-two as Pogačar tightens his GC grip

Wout van Aert, Milan Vader and Andreas Leknessund attacked from the flag, with Paul Ourselin, Timo Roosen, Iván Cobo and Hannes Wilksch slipping away soon after. The peloton reeled in the second group before Vader was distanced from the front trio, leaving Van Aert and Leknessund to press on alone at the head of the race.

Alexey Lutsenko, Léo Bisiaux and Filippo Zana set off in pursuit, and an 18-rider chase group including Tobias Halland Johannessen and Eddie Dunbar bridged across on the Puerto de Remolcador. Johannessen, Lutsenko and Dunbar then joined the two leaders just before the summit, where Leknessund took maximum KOM points; on the Alto de Zucaina the five escapees pulled clear of both the shattered chase group and a peloton by now over two minutes back. Van Aert crested the Puerto de San Rafael first as the quintet's advantage grew to seven minutes on the bunch.

With 7km still to climb to Aramón Valdelinares, Leknessund distanced Dunbar and Lutsenko and rode clear alone for his first Grand Tour stage win, a fourth-ever Vuelta stage win for Norway on the day the country's king died. Johannessen came around Van Aert in the closing metres to complete the Uno-X Mobility one-two; Van Aert took third but the day's real prize, moving into the green jersey ahead of Pogačar.

Further down the climb, Felix Gall attacked the GC group with Pogačar and Enric Mas the only riders able to follow; when Pogačar countered, Gall and Mas were both dropped, the red jersey gaining 6 seconds on Gall and 16 on Mas at the line. The move stretches Pogačar's overall lead over Mas from 3:34 to 3:50 heading into the race's first rest day.

§ 02 · GC impact

Pogačar extends to 3:50 as Gall vaults ahead of the chasing pack

Tadej Pogačar retains red and stretches his lead to 3:50 over Enric Mas. Primož Roglič holds third at 4:50, with Felix Gall's late attack lifting him to fourth at 5:01, just 11 seconds behind Roglič. Sepp Kuss is fifth at 6:05, Oscar Onley sixth at 6:06, Richard Carapaz seventh at 6:12, Mattias Skjelmose eighth at 7:17, Harold Tejada ninth at 7:46 and Gregor Mühlberger tenth at 9:29.

§ 04 · Rider-by-rider · tracked riders

Storylines from the stage

🇧🇪 Wout van Aert Team Visma | Lease a Bike
STAGE HUNTER P3 +0:47

Van Aert attacked from the flag alongside Leknessund and Vader, driving the day's decisive move over the Puerto de Remolcador and cresting the Puerto de San Rafael first as the break's lead swelled to seven minutes. He held on through Leknessund's late solo winning move and Johannessen's late pass to finish third on the stage, but the ride's real reward was taking over the green jersey from Pogačar.

🇸🇮 Tadej Pogačar UAE Team Emirates - XRG
LEADER P19 +4:23

Pogačar followed Felix Gall's attack on the GC group alongside Enric Mas, then counter-attacked in the closing kilometres to distance both, gaining 6 seconds on Gall and 16 on Mas. The move stretched his overall lead over Mas from 3:34 to 3:50 heading into the race's first rest weekend, even as he ceded the green jersey to Van Aert on the day.

🇦🇺 Felix Gall Decathlon CMA CGM Team
LEADER P20 +4:29

Gall was the most aggressive of the GC contenders, attacking on the Valdelinares climb to put the group under pressure and briefly distancing Roglič and the rest. Pogačar and Mas followed before the Slovenian counter-attacked and clawed back 6 seconds in the final kilometres, but Gall's move still lifted him to fourth overall, just 11 seconds behind Roglič.

🇸🇮 Primož Roglič Red Bull - BORA - hansgrohe
LEADER P23 +4:52

Roglič was dropped when Gall and then Pogačar accelerated at the front of the GC group, finishing in a chase alongside Kuss and Onley, 29 seconds behind the stage-day GC group leaders. He remains third overall at 4:50, 11 seconds ahead of Gall's new fourth place.

🏳️ Oscar Onley Netcompany INEOS Cycling Team
LEADER P24 +4:52

Onley finished in the group with Roglič and Kuss, 4:52 behind stage winner Leknessund, and sits sixth overall at 6:06 as the GC picture takes shape after the race's first summit finish.

🏳️ Richard Carapaz EF Education - EasyPost
LEADER P30 +5:45

Carapaz lost further ground on the Valdelinares summit finish, finishing 5:45 down, and drops to seventh overall at 6:12, over six minutes behind Pogačar.

🇩🇪 Mattias Skjelmose Lidl - Trek
LEADER P28 +5:42
