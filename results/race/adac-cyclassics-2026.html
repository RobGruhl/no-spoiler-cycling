§ R · 1.UWT · Germany

ADAC Cyclassics
2026

Paul Magnier (Soudal Quick-Step) won the 29th ADAC Cyclassics in Hamburg with a dominant sprint from a reduced group, holding off Mike Teunissen (XDS Astana Team) and Laurence Pithie (Red Bull - BORA - hansgrohe) after 207km from Buxtehude. It was his first WorldTour one-day victory, backing up three Giro d'Italia stage wins earlier in the season, after a late split over the Waseberg cost several top sprinters their chance.

§ 01 · The race in 90 seconds

Magnier survives a Waseberg split to sprint clear in Hamburg

OPENING207km from Buxtehude to Hamburg, with five ascents of the cobbled Waseberg (700m at over 9% average) on the closing circuit. A six-rider break — Jonas Rutsch, Benjamin Thomas, Dries De Pooter, local rider Niklas Behrens, Loe van Belle and Filip Maciejczyk — built a lead of over four minutes before the bunch brought it back below two with 50km to go.

UNFOLDSThe gap kept shrinking through a series of counter-moves — brothers Tim and Gianni Vermeesch briefly went clear before being reeled in — and by 37km to go De Pooter was struggling to hold the break's pace. Rutsch and Thomas pushed on alone over the final Waseberg climb but were swallowed up by the peloton on the descent, with Isaac del Toro driving the chase, bringing the race back together with 14km left.

DECIDEDIn the finale a crash in a feed zone earlier in the race had already taken down Del Toro and Biniam Girmay (both continued unhurt), and on the run to the line the peloton split, with Jonathan Milan and Olav Kooij missing the front group as Soudal Quick-Step organised for Magnier.

FINALEMagnier was launched from 300m out and held off the chasing group by daylight, taking his seventh win of the season and his first at WorldTour one-day level. Mike Teunissen and Laurence Pithie completed the podium, with Paul Penhoët, Henri Uhlig, Milan Fretin, Noah Hobbs, Søren Wærenskjold, Huub Artz and Arnaud De Lie rounding out the top ten.

§ 06 · Aftermath

Magnier backs up his Giro form with a first one-day WorldTour win

Soudal Quick-Step set out to avoid a repeat of 2025, when an early break spoiled the sprinters' party — this time the team controlled the race from the front, reeled in the day's escapees, and delivered Magnier for the finish. A late split cost Milan and Kooij their chance, but Magnier's sprint was untouchable regardless, giving him his seventh win of the season and a signature victory ahead of the Deutschland Tour.
