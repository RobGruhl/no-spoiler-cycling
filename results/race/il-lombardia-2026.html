§ 01 · The race in 90 seconds

A 20-year-old solos to history on the Civiglio

OPENINGBefore the start in Bergamo, the peloton delayed the race by five minutes in a safety protest over recent rider deaths and injuries in the sport, with world champion Brandon McNulty and European champion Remco Evenepoel at the front of the bunch; Evenepoel took a microphone to speak on the group's behalf. Once underway, the 239km route to Como settled into an aggressive opening before a 14-rider breakaway — Ben O'Connor, Antonio Tiberi, Victor Campenaerts, George Bennett, Fausto Masnada, Sylvain Moniquet, Louis Vervaeke, Julien Bernard, Michael Leonard, Gijs Leemreize, Pau Marti, Maxime Decomble, Andrea Mifsud and Johannes Kulset — went clear and built a lead of a little over three minutes, kept in check by Red Bull - BORA - hansgrohe, UAE Team Emirates - XRG, Decathlon CMA CGM and Pinarello-Q36.5. O'Connor animated the move hardest, repeatedly lifting the pace on the Giovenzana and the Madonna del Ghisallo and splitting the break apart.

UNFOLDSThe Ghisallo produced the first move from the favourites' group as Matteo Jorgenson attacked after being launched by teammate Louis Barré, pulling Quinn Simmons, Brandon McNulty and Christian Scaroni clear and forcing Red Bull - BORA - hansgrohe to chase through Giulio Pellizzari and later Primož Roglič. The quartet was caught on the run into Como, but the effort had already begun to thin the support around the main contenders. On the first ascent of San Fermo della Battaglia, Pellizzari's pace split the favourites' group further, dropping Roglič, Jorgenson and Lenny Martínez as the six survivors of the original break held around 45 seconds through the first pass of the finish line.

DECIDEDThat gap vanished on the decisive climb of the Civiglio, 18km from the finish. UAE Team Emirates - XRG moved to the front with Adam Yates and McNulty still shepherding Isaac del Toro, but Paul Seixas attacked before the Mexican could use his teammates, sweeping up the remaining escapees and immediately putting Remco Evenepoel into trouble. Thomas Pidcock and Giulio Ciccone briefly matched Seixas's pace while Del Toro slipped backwards, but Seixas kept driving and shed Pidcock, then Ciccone, near the summit. Enric Mas, who had ridden much of the climb at his own pace, passed Pidcock and caught Ciccone just before the top, as Seixas crested with roughly 30 seconds in hand.

FINALESeixas defended his margin on the technical descent and extended it again on the short final climb of San Fermo della Battaglia, where Mas attacked Ciccone and rode clear alone to secure second place. Ciccone, with Ben Healy closing to within 12 seconds behind him, held on for third as Pidcock tried and failed to fight his way back into podium contention. Seixas crested the final climb with close to 50 seconds in hand and descended into Como to win solo by 48 seconds over Mas, becoming, at 20, the youngest winner in Il Lombardia's 120-edition history and handing Decathlon CMA CGM its first Monument victory — ending the five-year reign of Tadej Pogačar and UAE Team Emirates - XRG over the race.

§ 02 · Decisive moments

Where the race tilted

  1. 01 35 km to go Madonna del Ghisallo
    Jorgenson's long-range dig
    Launched by teammate Louis Barré, Matteo Jorgenson attacked on the Ghisallo with Quinn Simmons, Brandon McNulty and Christian Scaroni, forcing Red Bull - BORA - hansgrohe into a chase that used up support around the main favourites before the move was caught.
  2. 02 18 km to go Civiglio
    Seixas rides everyone off his wheel
    Paul Seixas attacked on the Civiglio, absorbing the last of the breakaway and immediately distancing Remco Evenepoel and Isaac del Toro; only Thomas Pidcock and Giulio Ciccone could initially follow before both were shed near the summit.
  3. 03 4 km to go San Fermo della Battaglia
    Mas drops Ciccone for second
    Having fought back from behind, Enric Mas attacked Giulio Ciccone on the final climb and rode clear to secure second place, with Ciccone holding on for third as Ben Healy closed to within 12 seconds of him from the chase.
  4. 04 Finish line, Como
    Youngest winner in the Monument's history
    Seixas crossed the line solo in 5:49:28, 48 seconds clear of Mas, becoming the youngest Il Lombardia winner ever at 20 and ending Tadej Pogačar and UAE Team Emirates - XRG's five-year hold on the race.
§ 03 · Team-by-team

Who pressed, who missed

Decathlon CMA CGM Team
Seixas wins the team's first Monument

Seixas's solo victory, set up by a Ghisallo-area chase that cost rivals their support riders and sealed by his own Civiglio attack, gave Decathlon CMA CGM its first Monument title and closed out a breakout season that already included Classics podiums and stage-race wins.

Riders: SEIXAS Paul
Movistar Team
Mas climbs through for second

Enric Mas rode the Civiglio at his own pace rather than chasing immediately, then used that reserve to pass Thomas Pidcock, catch Giulio Ciccone and drop him on the final climb to secure the runner-up spot.

Lidl - Trek
Ciccone holds on for third

Ciccone was one of only two riders who could initially follow Seixas's Civiglio attack, lost contact near the summit, and then had to defend third against Mas and a fast-closing Ben Healy all the way to Como. Teammate Juan Ayuso, by contrast, never featured among the leaders and finished well down the field.

Riders: CICCONE Giulio, AYUSO Juan
Red Bull - BORA - hansgrohe
Evenepoel distanced on the Civiglio

Evenepoel, who had opened the day as the peloton's spokesman in the pre-start safety protest, could not respond when Seixas attacked on the Civiglio and settled into his own rhythm behind, finishing fourth after Giulio Pellizzari and Primož Roglič had already spent themselves chasing earlier moves.

Riders: EVENEPOEL Remco
UAE Team Emirates - XRG
Best Lombardia without Pogačar is fifth

With Tadej Pogačar sitting out while recovering from late-season collarbone surgery, UAE Team Emirates - XRG arrived at the Civiglio still shepherding Isaac del Toro with Adam Yates and Brandon McNulty, but Seixas attacked before they could use their numbers; Del Toro slipped back and McNulty ended up the team's best finisher in fifth, while Jan Christen did not finish and the team's five-year hold on the Monument ended.

Riders: MCNULTY Brandon, CHRISTEN Jan, POGAČAR Tadej
Pinarello-Q36.5 Pro Cycling Team
Pidcock closes but can't hold the podium

Pidcock was one of only two riders who could match Seixas's initial Civiglio acceleration, lost the wheel near the summit, and launched a late attack inside the final 3km trying to fight back into the podium picture, but finished sixth.

Riders: PIDCOCK Thomas
Team Visma | Lease a Bike
Jorgenson's long-range move is caught

Jorgenson's attack on the Ghisallo forced a response from the main contenders' teams and shaped the race's attrition, but the move itself was brought back before Como and he finished well down the classification, 23rd.

§ 06 · Aftermath

A new name on the Monument, and a season-long storyline resolved

Seixas's win closes out a breakout 2026 that had already included a Strade Bianche podium, an Itzulia Basque Country overall, a maiden Flèche Wallonne victory and a Liège-Bastogne-Liège podium — all behind Pogačar. With Pogačar absent, Seixas converted the form into his first Monument title at 20, the youngest winner in Il Lombardia's 120-year history, and gave Decathlon CMA CGM its first Monument win. The result ends UAE Team Emirates - XRG's five-year stranglehold on the race, with the team's best finisher, McNulty, over a minute behind in fifth and Isaac del Toro well back in 28th. The race was preceded by a five-minute peloton protest over rider safety, led publicly by Remco Evenepoel and world champion Brandon McNulty.
