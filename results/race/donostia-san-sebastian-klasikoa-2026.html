Donostia San Sebastian Klasikoa

Remco Evenepoel (Red Bull-BORA-hansgrohe) survived a mid-race bike-change scare to attack alone with Richard Carapaz on the Murgil-Tontorra and out-sprint him in San Sebastián, claiming a record fourth Klasikoa win and passing Marino Lejarreta as the race's sole record holder. § 01 · The race in 90 seconds

Evenepoel's fourth Klasikoa, forged in a mid-race chase

OPENINGAn eight-man break — Tim Rex, Iñigo Elosegui, Ådne Holter, Valentin Ferron, Lorenzo Quatrucci, Nicolás Alustiza, Nickolas Zukowsky and Joan Bou — went clear around 20-25 km in and built a lead approaching three minutes while Red Bull-BORA-hansgrohe controlled the peloton through the opening climbs.

UNFOLDSThe move started unravelling on the Jaizkibel, where Rex briefly rode clear alone before Bart Lemmen, Maxim Van Gils, Michael Leonard and Jay Vine bridged across from the bunch, giving Evenepoel a teammate up the road. The escapees were swept up near the summit, with Quatrucci having already banked the mountains points. Then, approaching the Erlaitz, Evenepoel was forced into a bike change and lost around 30 seconds without an immediate teammate to pace him back.

DECIDEDLouis Barré and Paul Double drove the pace on the Erlaitz itself before Giulio Ciccone took over and split the group further; Evenepoel had to chase twice — first back to a group containing Jan Christen, then solo on the descent — before teammate Giulio Pellizzari helped tow him back to the front on the run-in to the Murgil-Tontorra. Once reunited, Van Gils restored order for Red Bull-BORA-hansgrohe on the climb's lower slopes, and on the steepest ramps Evenepoel accelerated with only Richard Carapaz able to follow.

FINALEThe pair crested the Murgil-Tontorra roughly ten seconds clear of Louis Barré and defending champion Giulio Ciccone, then extended the gap on the descent into San Sebastián. Carapaz kept working until he was pushed to the front inside the final kilometre, setting up Evenepoel's sprint from his wheel. Behind, a chase group of around nineteen — including Ben Tulett, Christian Scaroni, Pello Bilbao, Ciccone, Jan Christen, Marc Hirschi, Léo Bisiaux and Felix Gall — arrived together 29 seconds down, with Tulett taking the group sprint for third.

§ 03 · Team-by-team

Who pressed, who missed

Red Bull - BORA - hansgrohe
Fourth Klasikoa despite a mechanical scare

A mid-race bike change threatened to end Evenepoel's bid before it started, but Maxim Van Gils and Giulio Pellizzari both worked to pace him back into contention, and once reunited the team controlled the front of the race into the Murgil-Tontorra. Evenepoel's decisive attack made it a record fourth Klasikoa win, with Van Gils also finishing inside the top 25.

Riders: PELLIZZARI Giulio
Bahrain Victorious
Bilbao fifth in the group sprint

Pello Bilbao made the front selection over the Murgil-Tontorra and rounded out the chase group's sprint for the minor placings, finishing fifth at 29 seconds — a solid result for the team's local card in a race he'd previously podiumed.

§ 06 · Aftermath

Evenepoel stands alone atop the Klasikoa roll of honour

Evenepoel's win — his fourth from four Klasikoa appearances (2019, 2022, 2023, 2026) — moves him clear of Marino Lejarreta's three victories as the race's outright record holder.
