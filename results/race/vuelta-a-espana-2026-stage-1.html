§ 01 · The stage

Pogačar wins a nail-biting home time trial by 9 hundredths of a second

The 9.4 km course through Monaco packed 25 corners into a technical loop from outside the Monte Carlo Casino, favouring explosive riders over pure time-trial specialists. Early benchmarks fell to a string of young riders — Jordan Labrosse, Matthew Brennan, Oscar Chamberlain, Léo Bisiaux and Callum Thornley all briefly held the fastest time — before Netcompany INEOS's Joshua Tarling, racing despite the recent death of his younger brother, set a new mark of 11:01.

National time-trial champion Ethan Hayter then went four seconds quicker than Tarling to take over the hot seat. Christophe Laporte and Finn Fisher-Black came close but not close enough, while Mads Pedersen and Wout van Aert both conceded time on the technical closing kilometres, finishing 16 and 9 seconds down respectively.

Starting second-to-last, Tadej Pogačar — racing on streets he knows as his adopted home — was only marginally down on Hayter's time through the course before edging the win by nine hundredths of a second at the line, taking the stage and the first red jersey of the race. Primož Roglič, starting last, also produced a strong ride but conceded over 20 seconds to his fellow Slovenian.

§ 02 · GC impact

Pogačar leads by the narrowest of margins after stage 1

Tadej Pogačar leads Ethan Hayter by nine hundredths of a second, with Joshua Tarling third at +0:04. Wout van Aert sits 8th at +0:09, Jay Vine 13th at +0:14, Mads Pedersen 15th and Matthew Brennan 16th both at +0:16, and João Almeida 18th at +0:18. Among the GC favourites further back, Primož Roglič is +0:22, Mattias Skjelmose +0:29, Richard Carapaz +0:33, Felix Gall +0:46 and Oscar Onley +0:50 — all conceding time on a course that suited punchy specialists over three-week climbers.
