§ 01 · The race in 90 seconds

Evenepoel out-sprints Ciccone after a 28km two-up move

OPENINGThe 15th Grand Prix Cycliste de Québec covered 206km on the 10.3km old-town circuit, tackled 20 times. A six-man break of Nikias Arndt, Jonas Rutsch, Samuel Boardman, Aivaris Mikutis, Frank van den Broek and Storm Ingebrigtsen went clear early and briefly built more than three minutes, but the gap came down steadily as the WorldTour field jostled for position on the punchy circuit.

UNFOLDSThe break was caught with 57km to go and the race turned tactical and fragmented, with Uno-X repeatedly splitting the front group on the circuit's short climbs. With just over eight laps left Quinn Simmons, Ben Healy, Bart Lemmen, Lennert Van Eetvelt and Maxim Van Gils launched a chase move, and with four laps to go Paul Seixas attacked, drawing Simmons, Van Gils, Ben Healy, Juan Ayuso, Matteo Jorgenson and Tom Pidcock across before Remco Evenepoel also bridged. Evenepoel went again with 42km to go alongside Seixas, signalling the start of the decisive phase.

DECIDEDWith 28km left Evenepoel accelerated on a flat sector of the circuit with Giulio Ciccone on his wheel; Anthon Charmig, Alessandro Pinarello and Alberto Bettiol were the only ones able to react immediately. The move caught several favourites — including Isaac del Toro, Paul Seixas and Tom Pidcock — out of position. Pinarello and Bettiol were dropped from the front trio as Evenepoel and Ciccone drove the pace, with UAE Team Emirates-XRG sending Jhonatan Narváez and Brandon McNulty to the front of the chase to try to bring del Toro back into contention, without success.

FINALEEvenepoel and Ciccone entered the final lap together with Charmig, who was dropped early on the Côte de la Montagne when Evenepoel accelerated. Evenepoel opened his sprint from distance on the uphill false-flat finish and held off Ciccone to the line. Behind, Tom Pidcock bridged across to Quinn Simmons late to salvage fourth.

§ 03 · Team-by-team

Who pressed, who missed

Red Bull - BORA - hansgrohe
Evenepoel wins

Evenepoel timed the decisive move perfectly, going clear with Ciccone 28km out and controlling the finale before beating him in the two-up sprint — a confidence-building win ahead of the World Championships in nearby Montréal. Schachmann also placed inside the top ten.

Riders: EVENEPOEL Remco
Lidl - Trek
Ciccone second, Ayuso caught out

Ciccone matched Evenepoel's winning move and held on for second in the sprint, a strong day for the Italian. Teammate Juan Ayuso, however, was among the favourites out of position when the decisive attack went and never got back into podium contention.

Riders: CICCONE Giulio, AYUSO Juan
UAE Team Emirates - XRG
Del Toro loses contact, finishes 55th

Isaac del Toro sat among the favourites for most of the race but was caught out of position when Evenepoel's move went clear 28km from the finish. Jhonatan Narváez and Brandon McNulty worked to pace him back into contention, but the chase lost cohesion on the closing laps and del Toro fell away to finish 55th.

Riders: DEL TORO Isaac
Pinarello-Q36.5 Pro Cycling Team
Pidcock salvages fourth

Pidcock was also dropped when the winning move went clear but fought back through the finale, bridging to Quinn Simmons late on to take fourth, the best result among the group of favourites who missed the decisive split.

§ 04 · Rider-by-rider · tracked riders only

How each story played out

🇧🇪 Remco Evenepoel Red Bull - BORA - hansgrohe
LEADER P1 winner

Evenepoel bridged to the race's key move with 42km to go, then launched the decisive attack himself with 28km left, taking only Ciccone with him. He drove the pace through the finale, distanced Charmig on the Côte de la Montagne, and opened his sprint from long range on the uphill finish to hold off Ciccone for the win — a strong marker ahead of the World Championships road race in Montréal a week later.

§ 06 · Aftermath

Evenepoel peaks in Québec ahead of the Worlds

The win gives Evenepoel a significant confidence boost heading into the World Championships road race in Montréal, and the peloton heads there next for the second of the Canadian WorldTour double-header on a more climber-friendly course.
