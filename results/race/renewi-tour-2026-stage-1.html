§ 01 · The stage

Milan edges Kooij in a photo finish as the breakaway falls agonisingly short

The 21st Renewi Tour opened with a fast circuit stage around Diest that never got hard enough to rule out a sprint but stayed nervous throughout, with short bergs (Heuvelstraat, Kerkstraat, Grasbos) offering launch pads without creating a real selection. Five riders — Lionel Taminiaux, Dries De Bondt, Timo Kielich, Carl-Frederik Bévort and Quinten Hermans — formed the day's breakaway early and built a lead that touched nearly two minutes, holding the peloton at arm's length through the Green KM bonus sprints at Engsbergen, where De Bondt and Hermans banked the biggest hauls.

Rain arrived with around 67 km to go and turned the finale technical: Jonas Abrahamsen, Oliver Naesen and Pier-André Côté all crashed in the wet closing kilometres as the bunch tried to organise a chase on narrow, slick roads. The break kept riding smart and stayed clear deep into the finale — still 18 seconds up with 2 km remaining — before hesitation among the five as the finish neared let the sprinters' trains close the gap and catch them just before the flamme rouge.

What followed was a drag race between the fastest survivors of a chaotic day. Kooij and Milan hit the line together, the gap so tight the result needed the photo, but Milan was confirmed the winner by the width of a tyre, giving Lidl - Trek the opening stage and the first leader's jersey of the race. Merlier and Philipsen completed the sprint podium places just behind.

§ 02 · GC impact

Milan in yellow after Diest

Milan's stage win and 10-second bonus put him into the overall lead. Kooij and Dries De Bondt sit 4 seconds back, with Quinten Hermans fourth at +5s after the breakaway quintet's Green KM bonus sprints trimmed their GC deficits despite finishing well down on the stage. Merlier is fifth overall at +6s, Timo Kielich sixth at +7s, and Taminiaux/Bévort share seventh at +8s. Philipsen, without bonus seconds, sits ninth at +10s heading into Thursday's flat run to the Netherlands and Friday's 13 km time trial.

§ 04 · Rider-by-rider · tracked riders

Storylines from the stage

🇮🇹 Jonathan Milan Lidl - Trek
LEADER P1 —

Milan stayed out of trouble through a rain-lashed, crash-affected finale and was perfectly placed once the five-man breakaway was reeled in just before the flamme rouge. In a chaotic, leadout-less sprint he found the front with around 100 m to go and held off a fast-closing Kooij by the narrowest of margins, confirmed only by the photo. The win, following on from his form at the Tour de Pologne, gives him the stage and the race's first leader's jersey.

🇳🇱 Olav Kooij Decathlon CMA CGM Team
STAGE HUNTER P2 s.t.

Kooij navigated the wet, crash-marred finale and launched a strong sprint once the breakaway was caught, closing hard on Milan in the final metres. The margin was so small the stage result needed a photo, and Kooij came out on the wrong side of it by a tyre's width — a bonus-seconds swing that leaves him second overall, 4 seconds off yellow.

🇧🇪 Tim Merlier Soudal Quick-Step
STAGE HUNTER P3 s.t.

Merlier survived the rain and the late crashes to contest the bunch sprint once the break was caught, rounding out the podium behind Milan and Kooij. He sits fifth overall, 6 seconds off the lead heading into the rest of the opening week.

🇧🇪 Jasper Philipsen Alpecin - Premier Tech
STAGE HUNTER P4 s.t.

Philipsen came through the chaotic, crash-affected finale to finish fourth in the sprint, just off the podium. Without bonus seconds from the day's breakaway sprints, he sits ninth overall, 10 seconds back.

🇧🇪 Arnaud De Lie Lotto Intermarché
STAGE HUNTER P10 s.t.

De Lie made it through the rain and the crashes in the leading group and was among the sprinters contesting the finish once the escapees were caught, rounding out the day inside the top ten on home Belgian roads.
