§ 01 · The race in 90 seconds

Pogačar dominates, then crashes out; Mas inherits red and builds a lead over Roglič and Gall

OPENINGThe 2026 Vuelta a España began in Monaco with a short, technical 9.4 km individual time trial that Tadej Pogačar won by nine hundredths of a second over Ethan Hayter, taking the race's first red jersey. Grand Tour debutant Matthew Brennan won the uphill sprint in Manosque on stage 2 after Wout van Aert's late attack for the jersey was caught, and the first summit finish at Font-Romeu on stage 3 was cancelled mid-stage when a hailstorm hit the Col de Mont-Louis, with no winner declared.

UNFOLDSStage 4 in Andorra was the race-defining day of the first week: Pogačar attacked alone on the Collada de Beixalis with 50 km to go, rode solo over the Coll d'Ordino and won by 2:39, opening a 3:21 overall lead on Primož Roglič. Brennan took a second sprint win on a windy Catalan stage 5, then Pogačar attacked on the gravel of the Puerto de Bartolo on stage 6, was caught by Enric Mas before the summit and beat him in a two-up sprint in Castelló for a third stage win. Andreas Leknessund led a Uno-X one-two at Aramón Valdelinares on stage 7 while Van Aert took the green jersey and Pogačar counter-attacked the GC group to stretch his lead over Mas to 3:50. Stage 8 to Xeraco changed everything. Roughly 32 km from the finish, on the approach to the Puerto de Barx, Pogačar went down hard in a peloton crash that also caught Ben Tulett. He tried to remount but could not continue and left the race by ambulance with a concussion, a displaced collarbone fracture and a stable neck fracture, the first Grand Tour abandon of his career. Bryan Coquard out-sprinted Mads Pedersen for the stage, and Mas became race leader for the first time in his career, 1:00 ahead of Roglič and 1:11 ahead of Felix Gall. Mas answered the pressure immediately, attacking inside the final 3 km of the Alto de Aitana queen stage on stage 9 to win and extend his lead to 1:18, with only Oscar Onley able to follow. After the rest day, Bastien Tronchon surprised the sprinters in Elche de la Sierra and Brennan completed a hat-trick in Lorca off a Van Aert lead-out, while Pedersen left the race with illness. On stage 12 Jakob Omrzel soloed to a breakthrough win on Calar Alto as Mas dropped Roglič and Gall on the final climb to push his lead to 1:45; Onley crashed early and slipped to fifth. Van Aert finally took his own stage on stage 13 into Loja, out-sprinting Valentin Paret-Peintre from a two-man move after Brennan neutralised the breakaway's attacks.

DECIDEDNot yet decided. After 13 of 21 stages Enric Mas leads Primož Roglič by 1:45 and Felix Gall by 2:06, with Richard Carapaz (+3:53), Oscar Onley (+4:29) and Jakob Omrzel (+4:59) next. The Sierra de la Pandera summit finish, the Sierra Nevada mountains and the 32 km Jerez time trial are still to come.

FINALENot yet raced. The route continues through Andalusia before finishing in Madrid on 13 September.

§ 02 · Decisive moments

Where the race tilted

  1. 01 Monaco (Stage 1, ITT)
    Pogačar wins the opening time trial by 0.09 seconds
    On a technical, corner-heavy 9.4 km course through Monaco, Tadej Pogačar started second-to-last and edged out Ethan Hayter by nine hundredths of a second to take the stage and the race's first red jersey.
  2. 02 50 km to go Collada de Beixalis (Stage 4, Andorra)
    Pogačar's 50 km solo raid blows the GC apart
    Pogačar attacked on gradients of up to 16 percent with 50 km to go, rode alone over the Coll d'Ordino and won by 2:39 on the chasing group, turning a nine-second lead into a 3:21 advantage over Roglič after just four stages.
  3. 03 32 km to go Approach to the Puerto de Barx (Stage 8)
    The race leader crashes out
    A heavy peloton crash roughly 32 km from the finish in Xeraco left Pogačar with a concussion and a broken collarbone. He tried to remount but was withdrawn by ambulance, handing the red jersey to Enric Mas and resetting a race Pogačar had led by 3:50.
  4. 04 3 km to go Alto de Aitana (Stage 9)
    Mas wins the queen stage in his first day in red
    Rather than defend, Enric Mas attacked inside the final 3 km of the week-one queen stage, dropped every rival except Oscar Onley and took the stage to extend his lead to 1:18 over Roglič.
  5. 05 2 km to go Calar Alto (Stage 12)
    Mas drops Roglič and Gall to push his lead past 1:45
    On the final ramps of Calar Alto, Mas accelerated away from Roglič and Gall while Jakob Omrzel won the stage from the break, stretching the overall lead to 1:45 with the Sierra de la Pandera and Sierra Nevada still ahead.
§ 04 · Rider-by-rider · tracked riders only

How each story played out

🇸🇮 Tadej Pogačar UAE Team Emirates - XRG
LEADER INCIDENT DNF (stage 8)

Pogačar won the Monaco time trial, the 50 km Andorra raid on stage 4 and the gravel finale in Castelló on stage 6, and had stretched his overall lead to 3:50 by stage 7 in what was shaping up as a crushing first Vuelta victory. A heavy peloton crash on the flat run-in to Xeraco on stage 8 ended it: a concussion, a displaced collarbone fracture and a stable neck fracture forced the first Grand Tour abandon of his career. He had collarbone surgery on 31 August.

On stage 13 he returned the favour, riding in the break and shutting down every attack so Van Aert could win.

§ 05 · Race incidents

Crashes, abandons, controversy

CRASH · 32 km · Stage 8, approach to the Puerto de Barx

Heavy peloton crash roughly 32 km from the finish at Xeraco, also involving Ben Tulett. Pogačar hit the tarmac hard, suffering a concussion, a displaced left collarbone fracture and a stable C7 vertebra fracture; he underwent collarbone surgery on 31 August.

CRASH · 131 km · Stage 12

Onley slid out on a roundabout about 131 km from the finish, remounted and chased back to the bunch, then lost further time on Calar Alto and dropped from fourth to fifth overall.

ABANDON · 32 km

POGAČAR Tadej — Withdrawn by ambulance on stage 8 after a heavy crash with a suspected broken collarbone and a head injury while leading the race by 3:50 — the first Grand Tour abandon of his career.

ABANDON

PEDERSEN Mads — Did not start stage 11 after withdrawing with illness.

§ 06 · Aftermath

Thirteen stages down, a race reset around Enric Mas

As of the evening of 4 September 2026, after stage 13, Enric Mas leads the Vuelta a España by 1:45 over Primož Roglič and 2:06 over Felix Gall, with Richard Carapaz, Oscar Onley and Jakob Omrzel completing the top six. Tadej Pogačar, who had led by 3:50 with three stage wins, abandoned after a heavy crash on stage 8 and underwent collarbone surgery on 31 August; his team has not yet said whether he will race again this season. Wout van Aert leads the points classification from teammate Matthew Brennan. The race continues with the Sierra de la Pandera summit finish on stage 14 before the Sierra Nevada mountains, the Jerez time trial and the finish in Madrid on 13 September.
