Stage 2
Renewi Tour 2026

Jasper Philipsen (Alpecin - Premier Tech) won a hectic, crosswind-hit stage 2 into Ardooie and inherited the race lead after overnight leader Jonathan Milan was swept up in a mass crash and lapped by the peloton, losing more than six minutes. § 01 · The stage

Milan's crash-marred day hands Philipsen the yellow jersey in Ardooie

The 173.9 km run from Blankenberge to Ardooie was flat on paper but nervous from the start, with crosswinds along the coastal roads threatening splits and keeping the bunch strung out and alert well before the finale.

With roughly 67 km to go, a front-to-rear wheel contact involving Tobias Bayer triggered a mass crash that brought down a large section of the peloton on a narrow, straight stretch of road — race leader Jonathan Milan among the worst affected. Milan remounted but repeatedly examined his wrist and struggled to hold his handlebars steadily; he continued alongside teammate Simone Consonni, but their deficit grew past six minutes, and once the race reached the finishing circuit in Ardooie the gap had grown so large that the peloton approached them from behind. Officials asked the Lidl - Trek pair to stop briefly at the roadside to let the main group pass before waving them back onto the course to complete the remaining circuit; despite the crash and the delay, Milan reached the finish within the time limit. Amaury Capiot, who abandoned after the same crash, was confirmed to have suffered a broken collarbone and fractured ribs; Nico Denz also failed to finish after the peloton's next passage of the line.

At the front, Decathlon CMA CGM lifted the pace beneath the flamme rouge as the sprint trains reorganised; Olav Kooij briefly lost his teammates through a bend before recovering position behind Dylan Groenewegen. Tim Merlier's chances were complicated when lead-out man Bert Van Lerberghe suffered a mechanical with 9 km to go, and Merlier lost further ground amid the jostling inside the final kilometre. Lukas Kubis led the bunch into the final 700 m before Philipsen took control from the front, launching his sprint at around 400 m and holding it to the line for his 65th career win — and his third stage victory in Ardooie.

Milan's collapse reshaped the general classification: Philipsen's stage bonus vaulted him from ninth to the overall lead, four seconds clear of Kooij and De Bondt, with the race turning immediately to Friday's hilly test around Geraardsbergen.

§ 02 · GC impact

Philipsen inherits yellow as Milan's race unravels

Philipsen leads by 4 seconds over both Olav Kooij and Dries De Bondt, with Quinten Hermans a further second back at +5. Tim Merlier and Søren Wærenskjold share sixth at +6 — Wærenskjold the day's biggest mover, up 65 places after avoiding the crash. Mike Teunissen, Arnaud De Lie and Matteo Trentin round out the top nine at +7 on Green Kilometre bonus seconds, with Timo Kielich tenth. Jonathan Milan, who led after stage 1, fell out of GC contention after losing more than six minutes in the crash and being lapped by the peloton before the finish.

§ 04 · Rider-by-rider · tracked riders

Storylines from the stage

🇧🇪 Jasper Philipsen Alpecin - Premier Tech
LEADER P1 —

Philipsen stayed clear of the crash that wrecked the day for the overnight leader and was perfectly placed for the reduced bunch sprint into Ardooie. He launched from the front at around 400 m to go and held off the chasers to take his 65th career win and third Ardooie stage success. The ten-second winner's bonus, added to his existing GC deficit, vaulted him from ninth overall into the race lead.

🇮🇹 Jonathan Milan Lidl - Trek
LEADER INCIDENT +6:00 or more

The overnight race leader was caught in a mass crash roughly 67 km from the finish, triggered by a wheel contact involving another rider. Milan remounted but repeatedly examined an injured wrist and struggled to hold the handlebars, falling back alongside teammate Simone Consonni. Their deficit grew past six minutes, and once the peloton caught them on the finishing circuit officials had the pair stop briefly at the roadside to let the group pass before waving them back onto the course — an extraordinary end to a one-day defence of the yellow jersey he won in Diest, though he did reach the finish within the time limit.
