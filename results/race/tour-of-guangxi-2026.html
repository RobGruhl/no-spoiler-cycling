§ 01 · The race in 90 seconds

Widar beats Pidcock on the final climb to deny Magnier a fairytale title defence

OPENINGThe 2026 Tour of Guangxi, the final WorldTour stage race of the season, began with a flat 149.4km loop out of and back into Chongzuo — the clearest sprint opportunity of the week before the race turns hillier and then mountainous later on. Neilson Powless, Fredrik Dversnes and Patrick Gamper formed the day's breakaway early and held a gap of around 1:30 through the first 100km, with Dversnes mopping up both intermediate bonus sprints along the way.

UNFOLDSSoudal Quick-Step and Decathlon CMA CGM Team shared the chase behind on stage 1, with Red Bull - BORA - hansgrohe also helping for Jordi Meeus. The break's lead fell below a minute with 23km to go and was caught with about 8km remaining, setting up the expected fight between the sprint trains. Stage 2's 179.7km run from Chongzuo to Jingxi was far less settled: an early three-rider gap was reduced, counter-attacked and reshuffled repeatedly, with Timo De Jong, Dries De Bondt and Jonas Rickaert each surfacing at the front before being pulled back. The categorised National Road climb (8.5km at 3.4%), 37km from the line, reduced both the escape and the rear of the peloton at once, and a late six-rider move featuring Afonso Eulálio went clear before being brought back inside the final 10km. Stage 3, at 212.3km from Jingxi to Bama, was the longest and toughest day yet: Jasha Sütterlin, Tobias Bayer and Ruidong Wang built a gap of nearly six minutes, with Wang mopping up the day's mountain points to take the polka-dot jersey, before Soudal Quick-Step and Decathlon CMA CGM Team reeled the move in with 19km to go. Stage 4's 165.7km from Bama to Tian'e kept the climbing going — Baptiste Veistroffer, Dries De Bondt, Robbe Ghys and Enzo Paleni led from the front for most of the day, and a late counter-attack from Kévin Vauquelin and Louis Barré nearly bridged across before the peloton's descent swept everyone up inside the final 14km. Stage 5's 170.1km from Jinchengjiang to Yizhou looked like a straightforward sprint stage on paper but packed nearly 2,400m of climbing into its first half; a six-rider move featuring Davide Formolo and Baptiste Veistroffer was finally reeled in with 14km to go, setting up a bunch sprint. Stage 6, the 165.8km queen stage from Yizhou to the Nongla summit finish, saw an early break of Jonas Rickaert, Stan Dewulf, Laurenz Rex and Yutao Shen dismantled in stages over the closing 25km as Julius Johansen and UAE Team Emirates - XRG drove the chase, before a string of late attacks from Rémy Rochas, Kévin Vauquelin and Peter Øxenberg set up the decisive climb.

DECIDEDOn stage 1, Decathlon CMA CGM Team delivered the finale's strongest lead-out for Olav Kooij, but he was boxed in during the last kilometre and never got his sprint started, clipping race winner Paul Magnier and losing his line entirely. On stage 2, it was Magnier's turn to be caught short: Soudal Quick-Step lost his lead-out man before the sprint, forcing him to launch into a headwind with 300m to go. Frits Biesterbos sat on his wheel and timed his own effort to come around him right at the line, with Kooij charging hardest of all from further back but running out of road. Stage 3 turned on a single moment: with Paul Magnier perfectly placed to contest a second win of the week, his foot slipped out of the pedal with 150m to go, handing Milan Fretin a clear run to the line and, on the overall countback, the race lead to Frits Biesterbos. Stage 4 brought two more swings — Olav Kooij did not start after a run of sprints that hadn't gone his way, leaving Decathlon CMA CGM Team to turn to Cees Bol, while Magnier himself was briefly dropped from the bunch at 77km to go before fighting back to the front group and out-sprinting Bol in Tian'e to reclaim the overall lead. Stage 5 brought real danger rather than a swing: with 500m to go, Magnier was brought down in a crash just behind the sprint, but the race jury's final-kilometre neutralization credited him with the bunch's time, so his 10-second overall lead survived intact into the final stage — while Milan Fretin won the sprint for his second stage of the week. Stage 6 settled everything on the Nongla climb. Magnier, no climber, was dropped from the group of contenders early on the final ascent and lost the race lead for good. Ahead of him, the GC fight came down to the last 400m: Louis Barré's attack brought Tom Pidcock and Jarno Widar clear, Pidcock countered with 100m left, and Widar answered immediately to take both the stage and the overall title.

FINALEFour different stage winners in the first four days — Magnier (stage 1), Biesterbos (stage 2), Fretin, twice (stages 3 and 5) and Magnier again (stage 4) — gave way to a final-day gut punch: Magnier's 10-second lead, defended through a crash and a pedal slip, disappeared on the only climb that mattered. Jarno Widar's stage 6 win made him the race's fifth different stage winner and, combined with his overall title by nine seconds over Tom Pidcock and sixteen over Louis Barré, the first WorldTour winner of his career. Magnier finished the race 99th overall at +12:00, a complete reversal after leading or sharing the top of the general classification for four of the six days.

    Isolated in the finishing straight, Magnier timed his sprint to beat Welsford and Kanter, while Decathlon's Kooij was boxed in and missed the top 10 entirely.
  4. 04 37 km to go National Road climb (stage 2)
    The categorised climb splits the race
    An 8.5km climb at 3.4% dropped riders from both the day's escape and the back of the peloton, narrowing the field able to contest the finish.
  5. 05 0.3 km to go Jingxi finish (stage 2)
    Biesterbos denies Magnier the double
    Launching early after losing his lead-out, Magnier was passed by Biesterbos right at the line, with Kooij closing hardest of all from further back for third. Six riders shared the same finishing time.
  6. 06 0.15 km to go Bama finish (stage 3)
    Magnier's foot slips off the pedal
    Perfectly placed to contest a second stage win, Magnier's foot came out of the pedal mid-acceleration, letting Milan Fretin through for the win and costing Magnier the race lead on countback.
  7. 07 14 km to go Descent before Tian'e (stage 4)
    Counter-move swept up
    Kévin Vauquelin and Louis Barré's late attack nearly bridged to the day's breakaway, but a fast peloton descent reeled in both groups at once, setting up a reduced bunch sprint.
  8. 08 0.1 km to go Tian'e finish (stage 4)
    Magnier reclaims the lead
    With Olav Kooij absent, Decathlon's Cees Bol led the chase for the win but Magnier held him off to take his second stage of the week and the overall lead back from Biesterbos.
  9. 09 0.5 km to go Yizhou finish (stage 5)
    Magnier crashes but keeps the jersey
    Race leader Paul Magnier was brought down 500m from the line, clipped from behind in the sprint chaos; the race jury's final-kilometre neutralization credited him with the bunch's time, so his 10-second overall lead survived into the final stage even as Milan Fretin won the sprint.
  10. 10 0.4 km to go Nongla climb (stage 6)
    Barré's move sets up the finale
    With Magnier already dropped from the GC group on the lower slopes, Louis Barré's attack with 400m to go brought Tom Pidcock and Jarno Widar clear of the rest, turning the race into a direct fight for the overall title between the three of them.
  11. 11 0.1 km to go Nongla finish (stage 6)
    Widar counters Pidcock to win the stage and the title
    Pidcock attacked with 100m to go, but Widar answered immediately to come round him for his first WorldTour win, taking the stage by six seconds and the overall title by nine over Pidcock.
§ 03 · Team-by-team

Who pressed, who missed

Soudal Quick-Step
Two stage wins, lost the title on the last climb

Soudal Quick-Step left Magnier to find his own way through the stage 1 finale, which he did emphatically to take the opener and the first leader's jersey. A costly pedal slip cost him the stage 3 win and the race lead on countback, but he answered immediately on stage 4, fighting back from being dropped to win again and retake the overall lead. He survived a stage 5 crash with the jersey intact thanks to a neutralization, but the queen stage was always the team's exposure: with no climbing threat of his own on the Nongla summit finish, Magnier was dropped early and lost the race lead for good, finishing 99th overall at +12:00 after leading or sharing top spot on the GC for four of the six days.

Riders: MAGNIER Paul
Lotto Intermarché
Widar wins the queen stage and the overall

Lotto Intermarché's week built to a single moment: on the Nongla summit finish, 20-year-old Jarno Widar survived the late reshuffling, arrived in the winning three-man move, and countered Tom Pidcock's late attack to take the stage and, with it, the overall title by nine seconds — his first WorldTour victory, a week and a half after also beating Pidcock at Tre Valli Varesine.

Riders: WIDAR Jarno
Pinarello-Q36.5 Pro Cycling Team
Pidcock denied the overall by nine seconds

Tom Pidcock arrived at the Tour of Guangxi as the clear favourite for the overall and rode the queen stage to match that billing, launching the race's decisive attack with 100m to go on the Nongla climb. Jarno Widar had an answer ready, and Pidcock had to settle for second on the stage and on the final general classification, nine seconds short of the title.

Riders: PIDCOCK Tom
Cofidis
Fretin wins twice in the bunch sprints

Milan Fretin was the week's most prolific sprinter, winning stage 3 after Magnier's pedal slip and then stage 5 after Magnier's late crash, taking both of the race's bunch-sprint opportunities that came free of a summit finish.

Team Picnic PostNL
Stage win, one day in the lead

A squad that had recently weighed a voluntary WorldTour withdrawal got its first WorldTour win of the season when Frits Biesterbos, racing in his debut professional year, followed Magnier's wheel and came around him at the line on stage 2. Magnier's stage 3 mishap put Biesterbos into the overall lead on countback for a day, before Magnier's stage 4 win took it back.

Decathlon CMA CGM Team
Kooij struggles, Bol steps in

After near-misses for Olav Kooij on stages 1 and 2 and a quiet stage 3, the team's lead sprinter did not start stage 4 following his run of below-par placings. Cees Bol deputised and finished second behind Magnier, the team's best result of the week.

Riders: KOOIJ Olav
§ 04 · Rider-by-rider · tracked riders only

How each story played out

🇫🇷 Paul Magnier Soudal Quick-Step
LEADER INCIDENT +12:00

Magnier opened his title defence in Guangxi exactly as he finished the 2025 edition — with a stage win, timing his own sprint from an isolated position to hold off Welsford and Kanter and take the race's first leader's jersey. A day later, without a lead-out man in the finale, he had to launch into a headwind with 300m to go and was passed right at the line by Biesterbos, but his second place kept him in the overall lead. Stage 3 cost him both the stage and the lead when his foot slipped off the pedal with 150m to go, dropping him to Biesterbos on countback. He answered immediately on stage 4, fighting back into the main group after being dropped with 77km to go, and out-sprinting Cees Bol in Tian'e to reclaim the overall lead. Stage 5 brought real danger: brought down 500m from the line, he remounted and finished well back, but the race jury's neutralization credited him with the bunch's time, so his 10-second lead carried intact into the final stage. The queen stage up the Nongla climb was always his point of maximum exposure, and it proved decisive — dropped from the group of contenders early on the climb, he lost over twelve minutes and the race lead for good, finishing the title defence 99th overall at +12:00.

§ 06 · Aftermath

Widar's late counter-attack denies Magnier a title defence

""

—

Jarno Widar's win on the Nongla summit finish closed out the 2026 WorldTour season with his first win at the level, taking the Tour of Guangxi overall by nine seconds over Tom Pidcock and sixteen over Louis Barré. The result turned on contrasts: Paul Magnier had defended his overall lead through a pedal slip and a crash across the race's first five days, only to be dropped from the lead group early on the one climb that mattered, finishing the title defence 99th overall at +12:00. Milan Fretin (Cofidis) was the week's most successful sprinter with two stage wins, while Frits Biesterbos' single day in the race lead stood as Team Picnic PostNL's high point of a season-ending race that produced five different stage winners across six days.
