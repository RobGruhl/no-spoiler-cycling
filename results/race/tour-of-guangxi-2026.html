§ R · 2.UWT · China

Tour of Guangxi
2026

The final WorldTour stage race of the 2026 season has produced four different stage winners in four days. Paul Magnier (Soudal Quick-Step) won stages 1 and 4, Frits Biesterbos (Team Picnic PostNL) won stage 2, and Milan Fretin (Cofidis) won stage 3 after Magnier unclipped mid-sprint — handing Biesterbos the lead on countback for a day before Magnier reclaimed it outright on stage 4, where Olav Kooij did not start after a string of sprints that didn't go his way.

§ 01 · The race in 90 seconds

Four stages, four winners — Magnier retakes the lead with two days left

OPENINGThe 2026 Tour of Guangxi, the final WorldTour stage race of the season, began with a flat 149.4km loop out of and back into Chongzuo — the clearest sprint opportunity of the week before the race turns hillier and then mountainous later on. Neilson Powless, Fredrik Dversnes and Patrick Gamper formed the day's breakaway early and held a gap of around 1:30 through the first 100km, with Dversnes mopping up both intermediate bonus sprints along the way.

UNFOLDSSoudal Quick-Step and Decathlon CMA CGM Team shared the chase behind on stage 1, with Red Bull - BORA - hansgrohe also helping for Jordi Meeus. The break's lead fell below a minute with 23km to go and was caught with about 8km remaining, setting up the expected fight between the sprint trains. Stage 2's 179.7km run from Chongzuo to Jingxi was far less settled: an early three-rider gap was reduced, counter-attacked and reshuffled repeatedly, with Timo De Jong, Dries De Bondt and Jonas Rickaert each surfacing at the front before being pulled back. The categorised National Road climb (8.5km at 3.4%), 37km from the line, reduced both the escape and the rear of the peloton at once, and a late six-rider move featuring Afonso Eulálio went clear before being brought back inside the final 10km. Stage 3, at 212.3km from Jingxi to Bama, was the longest and toughest day yet: Jasha Sütterlin, Tobias Bayer and Ruidong Wang built a gap of nearly six minutes, with Wang mopping up the day's mountain points to take the polka-dot jersey, before Soudal Quick-Step and Decathlon CMA CGM Team reeled the move in with 19km to go. Stage 4's 165.7km from Bama to Tian'e kept the climbing going — Baptiste Veistroffer, Dries De Bondt, Robbe Ghys and Enzo Paleni led from the front for most of the day, and a late counter-attack from Kévin Vauquelin and Louis Barré nearly bridged across before the peloton's descent swept everyone up inside the final 14km.

DECIDEDOn stage 1, Decathlon CMA CGM Team delivered the finale's strongest lead-out for Olav Kooij, but he was boxed in during the last kilometre and never got his sprint started, clipping race winner Paul Magnier and losing his line entirely. On stage 2, it was Magnier's turn to be caught short: Soudal Quick-Step lost his lead-out man before the sprint, forcing him to launch into a headwind with 300m to go. Frits Biesterbos sat on his wheel and timed his own effort to come around him right at the line, with Kooij charging hardest of all from further back but running out of road. Stage 3 turned on a single moment: with Paul Magnier perfectly placed to contest a second win of the week, his foot slipped out of the pedal with 150m to go, handing Milan Fretin a clear run to the line and, on the overall countback, the race lead to Frits Biesterbos. Stage 4 brought two more swings — Olav Kooij did not start after a run of sprints that hadn't gone his way, leaving Decathlon CMA CGM Team to turn to Cees Bol, while Magnier himself was briefly dropped from the bunch at 77km to go before fighting back to the front group and out-sprinting Bol in Tian'e to reclaim the overall lead.

FINALEFour stages, four different winners: Magnier (stage 1), Biesterbos (stage 2), Fretin (stage 3) and Magnier again (stage 4). Biesterbos' single day atop the general classification, held only on the countback tiebreak after stage 3, ended the moment Magnier won stage 4 — he now leads outright on 14:59:06, with Biesterbos second at +0:10 and Kanter third at +0:16. Jordi Meeus's consistency across four stages has him fifth overall at +0:22, the GC still separated by seconds with two stages to go.

  3. 03 0.3 km to go Chongzuo finish (stage 1)

    Isolated in the finishing straight, Magnier timed his sprint to beat Welsford and Kanter, while Decathlon's Kooij was boxed in and missed the top 10 entirely.
  4. 04 37 km to go National Road climb (stage 2)
    The categorised climb splits the race
    An 8.5km climb at 3.4% dropped riders from both the day's escape and the back of the peloton, narrowing the field able to contest the finish.
  5. 05 0.3 km to go Jingxi finish (stage 2)
    Biesterbos denies Magnier the double
    Launching early after losing his lead-out, Magnier was passed by Biesterbos right at the line, with Kooij closing hardest of all from further back for third. Six riders shared the same finishing time.
  6. 06 0.15 km to go Bama finish (stage 3)
    Magnier's foot slips off the pedal
    Perfectly placed to contest a second stage win, Magnier's foot came out of the pedal mid-acceleration, letting Milan Fretin through for the win and costing Magnier the race lead on countback.
  7. 07 14 km to go Descent before Tian'e (stage 4)
    Counter-move swept up
    Kévin Vauquelin and Louis Barré's late attack nearly bridged to the day's breakaway, but a fast peloton descent reeled in both groups at once, setting up a reduced bunch sprint.
  8. 08 0.1 km to go Tian'e finish (stage 4)
    Magnier reclaims the lead
    With Olav Kooij absent, Decathlon's Cees Bol led the chase for the win but Magnier held him off to take his second stage of the week and the overall lead back from Biesterbos.
§ 03 · Team-by-team

Who pressed, who missed

Soudal Quick-Step
Two stage wins, leads with two days left

Soudal Quick-Step left Magnier to find his own way through the stage 1 finale, which he did emphatically to take the opener and the first leader's jersey. A costly pedal slip cost him the stage 3 win and the race lead on countback, but he answered immediately on stage 4, fighting back from being dropped to win again and retake the overall lead with two stages to go.

Riders: MAGNIER Paul
Team Picnic PostNL
Stage win, one day in the lead

A squad that had recently weighed a voluntary WorldTour withdrawal got its first WorldTour win of the season when Frits Biesterbos, racing in his debut professional year, followed Magnier's wheel and came around him at the line on stage 2. Magnier's stage 3 mishap put Biesterbos into the overall lead on countback for a day, before Magnier's stage 4 win took it back.

Decathlon CMA CGM Team
Kooij struggles, Bol steps in

After near-misses for Olav Kooij on stages 1 and 2 and a quiet stage 3, the team's lead sprinter did not start stage 4 following his run of below-par placings. Cees Bol deputised and finished second behind Magnier, the team's best result of the week.

§ 04 · Rider-by-rider · tracked riders only

How each story played out

🇫🇷 Paul Magnier Soudal Quick-Step
LEADER P1 race leader

Magnier opened his title defence in Guangxi exactly as he finished the 2025 edition — with a stage win, timing his own sprint from an isolated position to hold off Welsford and Kanter and take the race's first leader's jersey. A day later, without a lead-out man in the finale, he had to launch into a headwind with 300m to go and was passed right at the line by Biesterbos, but his second place kept him in the overall lead. Stage 3 cost him both the stage and the lead when his foot slipped off the pedal with 150m to go, dropping him to Biesterbos on countback. He answered immediately on stage 4, fighting back into the main group after being dropped with 77km to go, and out-sprinting Cees Bol in Tian'e to reclaim the overall lead with two stages remaining.

🇳🇱 Olav Kooij Decathlon CMA CGM Team
STAGE HUNTER INCIDENT DNS stage 4

Kooij had the best lead-out of the stage 1 finale courtesy of Decathlon CMA CGM Team, but was boxed in during the final kilometre and never opened his sprint, finishing 12th. He answered a day later on stage 2, closing fastest of all in the final metres from further back than the other sprinters, but ran out of road and had to settle for third on the same time as the winner. Stage 3 was quieter — he was boxed out on the technical run into Bama and finished well outside the top 10 for the first time — and he did not start stage 4 after the run of results that hadn't gone his way, with Cees Bol deputising in his place.

§ 06 · Aftermath

Four stages down, two to go

Paul Magnier leads the general classification after four stages, with Frits Biesterbos second at 10 seconds back and Max Kanter third at 16 seconds. The race heads into stage 5, a hilly run that again looks like a chance for the reduced group of sprinters who have survived the climbing so far, before the final stage 6 sprint finish closes out the race and the WorldTour season.
