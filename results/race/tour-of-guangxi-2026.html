§ 01 · The race in 90 seconds

Magnier opens his Guangxi defence with another sprint win

OPENINGThe 2026 Tour of Guangxi, the final WorldTour stage race of the season, began with a flat 149.4km loop out of and back into Chongzuo — the clearest sprint opportunity of the week before the race turns hillier and then mountainous later on. Neilson Powless, Fredrik Dversnes and Patrick Gamper formed the day's breakaway early and held a gap of around 1:30 through the first 100km, with Dversnes mopping up both intermediate bonus sprints along the way.

UNFOLDSSoudal Quick-Step and Decathlon CMA CGM Team shared the chase behind, with Red Bull - BORA - hansgrohe also helping for Jordi Meeus. The break's lead fell below a minute with 23km to go and was caught with about 8km remaining, setting up the expected fight between the sprint trains. Dries De Bondt and Rune Herregodts each tried late counters after the catch, but neither stuck.

DECIDEDDecathlon CMA CGM Team delivered the finale's strongest lead-out for Olav Kooij, but he was boxed in during the last kilometre and never got his sprint started, appearing to clip race winner Paul Magnier in the process and lose his line entirely.

FINALERacing from a more isolated position, Magnier found his own way through the reorganising bunch and timed his effort perfectly to hold off Sam Welsford and Max Kanter at the line, repeating the dominant form that won him every bunch sprint at the race in 2025. He takes the first race lead; because Dversnes banked both bonus sprints in the break, the overnight GC order isn't a simple copy of the stage result — Kanter and Dversnes share second at +4s, with Welsford and Powless at +6s and Gamper at +8s.

§ 06 · Aftermath

One stage down, five to go

Paul Magnier leads the general classification after stage 1, with Max Kanter and Fredrik Dversnes tied for second at 4 seconds back and Sam Welsford and Neilson Powless at 6 seconds. The race heads from Chongzuo to Jingxi on stage 2, a harder day that gives punchier riders a chance to test the pure sprinters before the terrain turns properly hilly and then mountainous later in the week.
