§ 01 · The race in 90 seconds

Magnier survives Biesterbos' surprise to keep the lead through two stages

OPENINGThe 2026 Tour of Guangxi, the final WorldTour stage race of the season, began with a flat 149.4km loop out of and back into Chongzuo — the clearest sprint opportunity of the week before the race turns hillier and then mountainous later on. Neilson Powless, Fredrik Dversnes and Patrick Gamper formed the day's breakaway early and held a gap of around 1:30 through the first 100km, with Dversnes mopping up both intermediate bonus sprints along the way.

UNFOLDSSoudal Quick-Step and Decathlon CMA CGM Team shared the chase behind on stage 1, with Red Bull - BORA - hansgrohe also helping for Jordi Meeus. The break's lead fell below a minute with 23km to go and was caught with about 8km remaining, setting up the expected fight between the sprint trains. Stage 2's 179.7km run from Chongzuo to Jingxi was far less settled: an early three-rider gap was reduced, counter-attacked and reshuffled repeatedly, with Timo De Jong, Dries De Bondt and Jonas Rickaert each surfacing at the front before being pulled back. The categorised National Road climb (8.5km at 3.4%), 37km from the line, reduced both the escape and the rear of the peloton at once, and a late six-rider move featuring Afonso Eulálio went clear before being brought back inside the final 10km.

DECIDEDOn stage 1, Decathlon CMA CGM Team delivered the finale's strongest lead-out for Olav Kooij, but he was boxed in during the last kilometre and never got his sprint started, clipping race winner Paul Magnier and losing his line entirely. On stage 2, it was Magnier's turn to be caught short: Soudal Quick-Step lost his lead-out man before the sprint, forcing him to launch into a headwind with 300m to go. Frits Biesterbos sat on his wheel and timed his own effort to come around him right at the line, with Kooij charging hardest of all from further back but running out of road.

FINALEMagnier's stage 1 win, repeating the dominant form that won him every bunch sprint at the 2025 race, took the first leader's jersey; Dversnes' bonus seconds meant the overnight order wasn't a simple copy of the result, with Kanter and Dversnes sharing second at +4s. Stage 2 compressed the whole field at the line — Biesterbos, Magnier, Kooij, Kanter, Froidevaux and Meeus all shared the same time, 3:45:19 — but Magnier's second place was enough to keep the race lead. Biesterbos' maiden WorldTour win moves him to second overall at +6s, with Welsford third at +10s. Afonso Eulálio's involvement in the stage 2 breakaway puts him into the race's mountains jersey.

§ 03 · Team-by-team

Who pressed, who missed

Soudal Quick-Step
Stage 1 win, stage 2 2nd, keeps the lead

Soudal Quick-Step left Magnier to find his own way through the stage 1 finale, which he did emphatically to take the opener and the first leader's jersey. On stage 2, losing his lead-out forced an early sprint, but his second place was still enough to defend the race lead into stage 3.

Riders: MAGNIER Paul
Team Picnic PostNL
Surprise stage 2 win

A squad that had recently weighed a voluntary WorldTour withdrawal got its first WorldTour win of the season when Frits Biesterbos, racing in his debut professional year, followed Magnier's wheel and came around him at the line on stage 2.

§ 04 · Rider-by-rider · tracked riders only

How each story played out

🇫🇷 Paul Magnier Soudal Quick-Step
LEADER P1 —

Magnier opened his title defence in Guangxi exactly as he finished the 2025 edition — with a stage win, timing his own sprint from an isolated position to hold off Welsford and Kanter and take the race's first leader's jersey. A day later, without a lead-out man in the finale, he had to launch into a headwind with 300m to go and was passed right at the line by Biesterbos — but his second place kept him in the overall lead heading into stage 3.

  • 149 kmWon the stage 1 bunch sprint in Chongzuo to take the race lead
  • 179 kmForced into an early sprint on stage 2, finishes 2nd but retains the race lead
🇳🇱 Olav Kooij Decathlon CMA CGM Team
STAGE HUNTER P12 s.t.

Kooij had the best lead-out of the stage 1 finale courtesy of Decathlon CMA CGM Team, but was boxed in during the final kilometre and never opened his sprint, finishing 12th. He answered a day later on stage 2, closing fastest of all in the final metres from further back than the other sprinters, but ran out of road and had to settle for third on the same time as the winner.

  • 149 kmBoxed in during the final kilometre of stage 1 despite a strong Decathlon lead-out
  • 179 kmFastest finisher in the closing metres of stage 2, takes 3rd

§ 06 · Aftermath

Two stages down, four to go

Paul Magnier leads the general classification after two stages, with Frits Biesterbos second at 6 seconds back and Sam Welsford third at 10 seconds. The race heads from Chongzuo into the longest stage of the tour on stage 3, 211km with several climbs in the final third — tough enough that a reduced-peloton sprint is possible but a straightforward bunch kick looks unlikely.
