§ 01 · The stage

Carapaz masters the queen stage and polka dots; Kuss crashes twice as Evenepoel gains on Pogačar.

Stage 20 was the final mountain day of the 2026 Tour — 171 kilometres from Le Bourg-d'Oisans to Alpe d'Huez via an exceptional route that packed in the Col de la Croix de Fer, Col du Télégraphe, Col du Galibier (the Souvenir Henri Desgrange and the race's highest point), Col de Sarenne, and then a final approach to Alpe d'Huez via the Sarenne descent — a completely different road from the classic 21-hairpin route used the day before.

The race exploded on the Croix de Fer. Juan Ayuso, shedding his GC ambitions after Stage 19's losses, attacked early and helped fracture the peloton into the day's key breakaway: Richard Carapaz, Sepp Kuss, Ayuso, Ben Healy, Jai Hindley, Matthew Riccitello, Thymen Arensman, Tobias Halland Johannessen, Davide Piganzoli, Valentin Paret-Peintre, and several Team Visma | Lease a Bike riders — Bruno Armirail and Matteo Jorgenson — who were there to deliver Kuss to the decisive climbs. Carapaz, wearing the polka-dot jersey after Stage 19, attacked 3.7 kilometres from the Croix de Fer summit to sweep the maximum 20 KOM points. Mads Pedersen made his own audacious move, bridging to the breakaway specifically to contest the intermediate sprint in Saint-Jean-de-Maurienne. He won it uncontested, moving 82 points clear of Jasper Philipsen — all but securing the green jersey before the penultimate day.

On the Galibier — the highest summit of the Tour and the day's defining selection — Carapaz attacked 5 kilometres from the top, blowing the lead group apart. Kuss, Hindley and Riccitello were briefly able to come back, but Carapaz cleared the summit first and collected another 20 KOM points. That put the mountains classification mathematically out of Paret-Peintre's reach. Behind, Pogačar set tempo in the yellow jersey group with Del Toro on his wheel, not to chase the break but to control the GC rivals and protect his teammate's third place and white jersey. Seixas and Martinez were able to follow, but the UAE pace thinned the field sharply.

On the Col de Sarenne, Kuss attacked first with 9 kilometres to the summit. He gradually opened a lead on Carapaz, Hindley and Ayuso — for a time looking set to win. In the GC group, Del Toro accelerated on the Sarenne, distancing Seixas while Evenepoel and Pogačar followed. At the Sarenne summit Kuss led by a handful of seconds over Carapaz.

Then came the stage's dramatic turn. Kuss crashed with 7 kilometres remaining but got up quickly and held the lead. Seconds later, a second crash brought him down again with 6 kilometres to go. Carapaz, still close behind, went past while Kuss was on the ground. The Ecuadorian opened the gap quickly and rode clear. Behind, Evenepoel launched from the GC group with 3 kilometres remaining, passed a remounting Kuss on the finishing straight and took second. Pogačar slowed momentarily to allow Del Toro to regain contact before the line — the Slovenian never threatened to challenge for stage honours, shepherding his teammate instead. Carapaz crossed the line alone: his second Tour stage win in three days, his third career Tour stage win, and the polka-dot jersey secured.

§ 04 · Rider-by-rider · tracked riders

Storylines from the stage

🏳️ Richard Carapaz EF Education - EasyPost
STAGE HUNTER P1 winner

A complete final-week campaign from Carapaz. After winning Stage 18 at Orcières-Merlette and taking the polka-dot jersey on Stage 19 despite being caught by Pogačar, he arrived at Stage 20 with both the jersey and the points lead to defend. He attacked for KOM points on the Croix de Fer (20 points, 3.7km from summit), the Télégraphe, and decisively on the Galibier — where he attacked 5km from the summit, splitting the breakaway and effectively putting the mountains classification out of reach for Valentin Paret-Peintre. On the Sarenne, Kuss went clear, but Carapaz kept chasing. When Kuss crashed twice in the final 7km, Carapaz had already been closing — he went past on the second crash, opened the gap immediately and rode solo to the line. His third career Tour stage win came wearing the jersey he had fought all week to secure.

🇧🇪 Remco Evenepoel Red Bull - BORA - hansgrohe
LEADER P2 +0:26

Evenepoel's final mountain day was his strongest since the Thonon-les-Bains time trial. He rode in control through the early climbs within the GC group and then launched an opportunistic attack from the Pogačar group with 3 kilometres remaining on the run to Alpe d'Huez. He passed a remounting Kuss on the finishing straight to take second place, cutting the GC gap to Pogačar by 0:45 — from 7:11 to 6:26. With only Paris remaining, second overall is now effectively confirmed. Jai Hindley, Evenepoel's Red Bull teammate, also delivered a strong day finishing 7th.

🇸🇮 Tadej Pogačar UAE Team Emirates - XRG
LEADER P4 +1:05

After his record-setting stage win on Stage 19, Pogačar's Stage 20 told a very different story: the yellow jersey riding in service of his teammate. From the Galibier onwards he set tempo with Del Toro on his wheel, not to drop rivals for personal glory but to protect UAE's podium architecture. When Del Toro attacked on the Sarenne and Seixas cracked, Pogačar modulated his effort to keep Del Toro covered, even slowing briefly near the line to help him regain contact. Losing 45 seconds to Evenepoel in GC terms matters little — the overall lead of 6:26 with only Paris remaining is insurmountable in any reasonable scenario.

🇲🇽 Isaac del Toro UAE Team Emirates - XRG
LEADER P5 +1:05

Del Toro was the day's protected rider and he responded when it mattered most. Decathlon CMA CGM sent Prodhomme and Riccitello to the front on the Sarenne to force a selection and compromise the white jersey. Del Toro answered by attacking — distancing Seixas, who was the very rider trying to take time on him. He finished 5th at the same time as Pogačar (+1:05), leaving his overall gap to Seixas at 2:14 (up from 1:56 before the stage). Third overall and the white jersey are now effectively guaranteed heading to Paris.

🇫🇷 Lenny Martínez Bahrain Victorious
LEADER P6 +1:07

After his astonishing ride to 2nd on Stage 19, Martínez maintained the momentum in the GC group on Stage 20. He finished 6th at just 1:07 from Carapaz — only 2 seconds behind Pogačar — effectively matching the yellow jersey on the day. His overall gap barely moved, from +13:00 to +13:02. He retains 5th in the GC and heads to Paris with his highest-ever Grand Tour finish in reach.

🇫🇷 Paul Seixas Decathlon CMA CGM Team
LEADER P10 +2:55

Stage 20 was Seixas's final chance to overhaul Del Toro for third place, and Decathlon CMA CGM went all-in — Prodhomme and Riccitello raised the pace on the Sarenne to force a split. Seixas initially followed, but Del Toro turned the attack around, and it was Seixas who cracked. He finished 10th in a group with Skjelmose and Ayuso at +2:55, losing 1:50 to Pogačar and falling to 4th at +11:56. Fourth overall in the Tour de France at 19 years old remains a generational result, but the podium opportunity has passed.

🇩🇪 Mattias Skjelmose Lidl-Trek
LEADER P8 +2:55

Skjelmose regrouped after Stage 19's losses and finished the Tour's final mountain stage respectably — 8th at +2:55, in the company of Seixas and Ayuso. He lost the same 1:50 as those two to Pogačar. He slips to 6th in GC at +14:59 — still a strong result for a Tour that was complicated by his teammate Ayuso's struggles in the Alps.

🏳️ Juan Ayuso Lidl-Trek
LEADER P9 +2:55

Ayuso went into the day's breakaway — helping to animate the race after his Stage 19 implosion — and drove the early split on the Croix de Fer. But once his breakaway work was done he fell back to the GC group and finished 9th at +2:55, the same time as Seixas and Skjelmose. He slips to 7th overall at +17:48, effectively ending his Tour with two successive Alpine setbacks. His breakaway animation gave the stage its early energy even if he could not compete at the front by the Sarenne.

🇩🇪 Mads Pedersen Lidl-Trek
STAGE HUNTER — n/a (finished with gruppetto)

Pedersen produced one of his most impressive riding performances — not on a flat stage, but by bridging to the breakaway group on the Col de la Croix de Fer specifically to contest the intermediate sprint in Saint-Jean-de-Maurienne. He won it uncontested, collecting 25 points to extend his lead over Jasper Philipsen to 82 points with 95 remaining available across the rest of the Tour. Barring a dramatic reversal on Stage 21, the green jersey is now mathematically safe. He predictably lost contact with the climbers after his sprint mission was complete.

🏳️ Matthew Riccitello Decathlon CMA CGM Team
HELPER P12 +7:57

Riccitello spent the day working in the breakaway for Decathlon CMA CGM's GC ambitions — riding tempo on the Galibier and later dropping back from the break to help set pace in the GC group on the Sarenne for Seixas's challenge against Del Toro. He finished 12th at +7:57, a creditable result after a full day of team work.

🏳️ Matteo Jorgenson Team Visma | Lease a Bike
HELPER P56 +35:10

Jorgenson sacrificed himself entirely for Kuss on Stage 20, riding tempo at the front of the breakaway on the early climbs before losing contact as the race grew harder. He finished 56th — his time gap reflecting total exhaustion — but his effort was critical to ensuring Kuss arrived at the Sarenne fresh enough to attack for the stage win.

🏳️ Tom Pidcock Pinarello Q36.5 Pro Cycling Team
LEADER INCIDENT n/a (outside top 20)

A second consecutive Alpine stage of heavy losses for Pidcock, who fell further behind the pace and lost approximately 7:58 to Pogačar. He drops to 9th in GC at +29:28, a position that reflects two days of uncharacteristic difficulties on the biggest climbs. No crash or incident was reported; the cause of his struggles across the two Alpe d'Huez stages remains unclear. His Tour ends effectively tomorrow with a processional stage into Paris.

⚠ Second successive Alpine stage with major losses to Pogačar — no mechanical or crash reported
