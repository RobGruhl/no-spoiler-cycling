§ 01 · The stage

Biesterbos denies Magnier the double in a compressed finish

The 179.7km run from Chongzuo to Jingxi was shaped less by one lasting breakaway than by a succession of short-lived moves. An early three-rider gap with 178km to go was reduced, counter-attacked and reshuffled repeatedly through the first half of the stage, with riders including Timo De Jong, Dries De Bondt and Jonas Rickaert surfacing at the front before being absorbed back into the peloton. The categorised National Road climb, 8.5km at 3.4%, arrived with 37km left and reduced both the escape and the rear of the bunch at once, dropping Liu Chenglu from the break and splintering the peloton behind. Jhonatan Narváez and Javier Romo countered over the top, and a six-rider Bahrain Victorious-led move featuring Afonso Eulálio went clear with 29km remaining before the race came back together. Kévin Vauquelin and Baptiste Veistroffer each tried late attacks inside the final 10km, but neither created a winning gap, funnelling a reduced front group to the line together. Soudal Quick-Step lost its lead-out before the sprint, forcing Magnier to launch into the headwind with 300m to go; Biesterbos sat on his wheel and came around him at the line, with Kooij charging hardest of all from behind but running out of road for the win. Biesterbos, Magnier, Kooij, Kanter, Froidevaux and Meeus all crossed in the same time, 3:45:19.

§ 02 · GC impact

Magnier retains the race lead; Biesterbos moves to second

Paul Magnier keeps the overall lead he took on stage 1. Frits Biesterbos's stage win lifts him to second overall, 6 seconds back, with Sam Welsford holding third at +10s. Afonso Eulálio's involvement in the day's late breakaway and the second climb sprint puts him in the race's mountains classification lead.

§ 04 · Rider-by-rider · tracked riders

Storylines from the stage

🇫🇷 Paul Magnier Soudal Quick-Step
LEADER P2 s.t.

Without a lead-out man in the final kilometre, Magnier had to launch his own sprint into a headwind with 300m to go rather than waiting for the ideal moment he had on stage 1. Biesterbos followed his wheel and came around him right at the line, but Magnier's second place was enough to keep the overall race lead heading into stage 3.

🇳🇱 Olav Kooij Decathlon CMA CGM Team
STAGE HUNTER P3 s.t.

A day after missing the top ten boxed in on stage 1, Kooij came from further back than both Magnier and Biesterbos and was still closing fastest of all at the line. Two more metres and he would have taken it; instead he had to settle for third, on the same time as the winner.

🇧🇪 Jordi Meeus Red Bull - BORA - hansgrohe
STAGE HUNTER P6 s.t.

Meeus stayed in touch through the National Road climb and the late attacks to contest the sprint from the same reduced group as the podium, crossing sixth on the same time as Biesterbos.
