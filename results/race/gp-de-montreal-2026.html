GP de Montréal
2026

Isaac del Toro (UAE Team Emirates - XRG) won the 15th Grand Prix Cycliste de Montréal, bridging to a late Paul Seixas attack on the final ascent of the Côte de la Camillien-Houde and beating him in an uphill two-up sprint. Brandon McNulty completed an all-UAE top two behind the winner in third, as Ben Healy's long-range solo effort set up the finale on the 214.4km, 16-lap Mont Royal circuit.

§ 01 · The race in 90 seconds

Del Toro bridges to Seixas on the final climb, wins the uphill sprint

OPENINGThe 15th Grand Prix Cycliste de Montréal covered 214.4km over 16 laps of the 13.5km Mont Royal circuit, which opens every lap with the Côte de la Camillien-Houde (1.7km at 7.9%). A nine-man break — Michal Kwiatkowski, Casper Pedersen, Ådne Holter, Anders Foldager, Pelayo Sánchez, Robbe Dhondt, Jérôme Gauthier, Robin Carpenter and Kieran Haug — went clear early and built a gap of around two minutes, with UAE Team Emirates-XRG, Red Bull-BORA-hansgrohe and Decathlon CMA CGM Team controlling the pace behind.

UNFOLDSThe break was caught with eight laps to go, and the race reset. Juan Ayuso launched a fresh attack with 84km left, and EF Education-EasyPost's Michael Leonard bridged across as the pair built a gap of over 20 seconds with five laps remaining. Isaac del Toro spent this phase struggling near the back of a peloton being paced by Team Visma | Lease a Bike and Red Bull-BORA-hansgrohe. With four laps to go Wilco Kelderman attacked and reeled the leaders back within range; Ben Healy then countered on the climb with Adam Yates and Maxim Van Gils, catching Kelderman as Leonard extended the move's gap toward 30 seconds.

DECIDEDInside the final 40km, on the climb, Healy attacked out of the lead group alone as Ayuso and Kelderman were dropped; he crested with Van Gils and Yates over 45 seconds back, while Tom Pidcock's pace in the chase whittled the favourites' group down to around 15 riders. On the penultimate lap's climb, Quinn Simmons kicked off the decisive selection behind Healy; Brandon McNulty bridged and countered, drawing Paul Seixas, Giulio Ciccone, Isaac del Toro, Afonso Eulálio, Tom Pidcock and a re-caught Adam Yates across. Remco Evenepoel missed the split — appearing to be blocked by a Lidl-Trek rider — and had to chase alone from the group behind, rejoining only as the race reached the final lap.

FINALEHealy held off the regrouped chasers by more than ten seconds onto the final lap before Seixas attacked out of the group on the Côte de la Camillien-Houde, caught and passed him near the top with only McNulty able to respond. Surging late over the summit, del Toro bridged across to Seixas and the pair built a lead of almost 30 seconds with 7km to go as Simmons and McNulty broke away behind in a separate chase for third. Seixas led out the sprint through the final bend, but del Toro came off his wheel to take the win, with McNulty holding off Simmons for third.

§ 03 · Team-by-team

Who pressed, who missed

UAE Team Emirates - XRG
Del Toro wins, McNulty third

Del Toro spent much of the race struggling near the back of the peloton before timing his winning move perfectly, bridging to Seixas on the final climb and beating him in the sprint — a major statement two weeks before the World Championships on the same circuit. Teammate Brandon McNulty added a third-place finish, giving UAE two of the top three spots.

Riders: DEL TORO Isaac, MCNULTY Brandon
Decathlon CMA CGM Team
Seixas second

Seixas produced the race's decisive attack on the final climb, catching and passing the long-range leader Ben Healy, but couldn't hold off del Toro's late bridge and was beaten in the uphill sprint for second.

Riders: SEIXAS Paul
EF Education - EasyPost
Healy animates, fades to ninth

Healy launched the race's key long-range move inside the final 40km, riding clear of the favourites and holding a lead of over 45 seconds before the group behind reorganised on the penultimate lap. He was caught and passed on the final climb, eventually finishing ninth after a day spent animating the race.

§ 04 · Rider-by-rider · tracked riders only

How each story played out

🇲🇽 Isaac del Toro UAE Team Emirates - XRG
LEADER P1 winner

Del Toro spent long stretches of the race struggling near the back of a peloton being driven hard by Visma and Red Bull, but timed his effort perfectly for the finale. He surged over the top of the final ascent of the Camillien-Houde to bridge across to Paul Seixas, then rode a controlled sprint from his wheel to win his first WorldTour one-day race — a major statement two weeks out from the World Championships on the same Montréal circuit.

  • 7 kmBridges to Seixas's attack over the top of the final climb
  • 0.1 kmComes off Seixas's wheel to win the uphill sprint
🇫🇷 Paul Seixas Decathlon CMA CGM Team
LEADER P2 s.t.

Seixas launched the race's decisive move on the final climb, catching and passing long-range leader Ben Healy with only Brandon McNulty briefly able to follow. He led the two-up move with del Toro through to the finish, doing much of the work on the uphill drags, but was beaten in the sprint after leading it out too early — a strong runner-up finish that sets up a rematch with del Toro at the Worlds.

§ 06 · Aftermath

Del Toro the form rider heading into the Worlds

The win puts del Toro in pole position two weeks out from the World Championships road race, held on essentially the same Mont Royal circuit. Seixas's runner-up finish confirms him as France's leading threat on the same course, while Evenepoel — the pre-race favourite after his GP de Québec win days earlier — will look to bounce back after missing the decisive move.
