§ 01 · The stage

Van Aert outsprints Romele and Nys as a shortened Córdoba stage goes to the break

With Andalusian temperatures forecast to soar, organisers shortened the stage from 189.7km to 110.2km overnight, cutting the early rolling terrain but leaving the closing Puerto Artafi climb and Córdoba's finishing circuit intact. A five-rider move containing Wout van Aert, Thibau Nys, Alessandro Romele, Ramses Debruyne and Andreas Leknessund went clear early and was never seriously challenged, with the main bunch content to let a stage-hunting group take the day the morning after the Sierra de la Pandera summit finish.

The break worked cleanly to the finish and reached the closing circuit through the historic centre together, setting up a straight sprint. Van Aert timed his effort perfectly to beat Romele and Nys, taking his third stage win of this Vuelta and further points towards the green jersey. Behind, the GC contenders were content to sit up and conserve energy a day after Pandera's fireworks, rolling home together 2:44 down with no changes among the overall favourites.

§ 02 · GC impact

GC frozen: Mas's lead over Gall and Roglič holds unchanged

With the breakaway taking the stage and the GC group finishing together, every position in the top ten carried over unchanged from stage 14. Enric Mas leads Felix Gall by 2:06 and Primož Roglič by 2:10, with Richard Carapaz fourth at 4:24 and Oscar Onley fifth at 5:44 as the race heads into its final week.

STAGE HUNTER P1

Van Aert made the day's decisive five-rider break from early on the shortened route and never had to work especially hard to stay away, with the peloton behind giving the move its blessing the day after the Sierra de la Pandera summit finish. In Córdoba's finishing circuit he judged his sprint perfectly, holding off Alessandro Romele and Thibau Nys for his third stage win of this Vuelta and more points towards a green jersey he already holds comfortably.
