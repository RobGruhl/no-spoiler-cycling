§ 01 · The race in 90 seconds

Milan's Diest win undone by an Ardooie crash as Philipsen takes over

OPENINGThe 21st edition of the Renewi Tour set off from Diest on a circuit stage that mixed repeated passages of the finish with short Flemish bergs — Heuvelstraat, Kerkstraat and Grasbos — never hard enough to force a real selection but awkward enough to keep the peloton nervous. Five riders — Lionel Taminiaux, Dries De Bondt, Timo Kielich, Carl-Frederik Bévort and Quinten Hermans — got clear early and built a lead approaching two minutes, banking bonus seconds at the Green KM intermediate sprints in Engsbergen.

UNFOLDSRain arrived with roughly 67 km to go on stage 1 and turned the finale technical and dangerous: Jonas Abrahamsen, Oliver Naesen and Pier-André Côté all crashed on the wet, narrow closing circuits. Despite the chaos behind them, the five leaders defended their advantage smartly, still 18 seconds clear with 2 km to go — until hesitation among the escapees let the sprinters' trains close the gap and swallow the break just before the flamme rouge, where Jonathan Milan out-sprinted Olav Kooij by a tyre's width to take the first yellow jersey. Stage 2's flat, crosswind-exposed run from Blankenberge to Ardooie stayed nervous throughout, and with roughly 67 km to go a mass crash — triggered by a wheel contact involving Tobias Bayer — brought down a large section of the bunch, race leader Milan among the worst affected. Milan remounted but struggled to hold his handlebars through an injured wrist; his deficit grew past six minutes, and with about 5 km left at the front, the peloton lapped him and teammate Simone Consonni on the finishing circuit, forcing them to pull aside.

DECIDEDNot yet decided. Two of five stages are complete; Philipsen inherited the race lead on bonus seconds after Milan's stage-2 misfortune, four seconds up on Olav Kooij and Dries De Bondt. The race now turns to Friday's 182 km stage from Celles to Geraardsbergen — the highest-rated profile of the week and the traditionally decisive Flemish-hills day.

FINALENot yet raced. Stages 3 (2026-08-21), 4 (2026-08-22) and 5 (2026-08-23) remain.

§ 02 · Decisive moments

Where the race tilted

  1. 01 Diest (Stage 1)
    Milan wins the photo finish, takes yellow
    A five-man breakaway was caught just inside the final kilometre after leading much of the rain-soaked stage; Jonathan Milan then out-sprinted Olav Kooij by the narrowest of margins to win stage 1 and the race's first leader's jersey.
  2. 02 67 km to go Between Blankenberge and Ardooie (Stage 2)
    Mass crash ends Milan's race lead
    A wheel contact involving Tobias Bayer triggered a mass crash roughly 67 km from the Ardooie finish. Race leader Jonathan Milan was among those most affected, losing more than six minutes and being lapped by the peloton before the finish — handing the lead to stage-2 winner Jasper Philipsen.

How each story played out

🇧🇪 Philipsen Jasper Alpecin - Premier Tech
LEADER P1 race leader

Philipsen stayed clear of stage 2's mass crash and won the reduced bunch sprint into Ardooie for his 65th career victory. The stage bonus, added to his existing deficit, vaulted him from ninth to the overall lead, four seconds up on Olav Kooij and Dries De Bondt with three stages remaining.

  • Stage 2: won the reduced bunch sprint in Ardooie and took over the race lead after Milan's crash
