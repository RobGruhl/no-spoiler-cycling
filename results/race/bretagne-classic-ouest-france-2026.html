§ 01 · The race in 90 seconds

Aranburu strikes late to claim the biggest win of his career

OPENINGThe 228km route from and to Plouay built toward its traditional finale: repeated ascents of the Côte de Ty Marrec on the closing circuit. French riders were the most combative through the finale, with Louis Barré launching repeated attacks in the last 20km across the climb's four ascents, joined by other home riders looking to spring a Breton winner in front of the local crowd.

UNFOLDSOn the final ascent of Ty Marrec, the day's remaining breakaway group — which had included Emil Herzog — was initially swallowed by the peloton, and for a while it looked as though the race would again be decided in a bunch sprint, as it had been a year earlier when Arnaud De Lie won. But Herzog, only 21, refused to accept that and attacked again around six kilometres from the finish, driving hard enough that roughly ten riders split clear over the top of the climb.

DECIDEDThe gap that opened behind Herzog's move grew on the flat run-in as confusion over who should chase let it stretch further. From within the chasing peloton, Aranburu accelerated on a small rise just before the final sprint and was never seen again, soloing to the line for his third win of the season and the twelfth of his career.

FINALEAranburu held his lead to the finish, with Alexander Kamp closing fastest for second and Alessandro Borgo taking third from the remnants of the late breakaway; Herzog, whose attack had set up the finale, came home fourth. The French attackers who had animated the finale — Paul Lapeira, Romain Grégoire and Alexandre Delettre among them — went unrewarded, finishing sixth, seventh and ninth. Aranburu called it the biggest win of his career, adding to Cofidis teammate Bryan Coquard's surprise Vuelta a España stage win the day before.

§ 03 · Team-by-team

Who pressed, who missed

Cofidis
Perfect week

A day after Bryan Coquard's surprise Vuelta a España stage win, Cofidis doubled up with Aranburu's biggest career victory. The team was the only one to place two riders in the decisive late breakaway (with Dylan Teuns), setting up Aranburu's winning move.

§ 06 · Aftermath

A career-best win for Aranburu

""

—

Aranburu's win is his third of the season and the most important of a career that has otherwise featured WorldTour stage wins at the Itzulia Basque Country. The result continues a strong week for Cofidis following Coquard's stage win at the Vuelta a España, while the home French contingent — Barré, Lapeira, Grégoire, Delettre and others — left Plouay frustrated after animating the finale without reward.
